# Follow the mandates: Austin's $10K shortlist

The strongest evidence-backed $10,000 business in metro Austin for someone with no special credential is **construction stormwater (SWPPP) inspection**. Texas's Construction General Permit requires every covered site, meaning one that disturbs an acre or more, to be inspected **at least every 14 days and within 24 hours of a half-inch rain**. The state requires no license to do this work, and the demand comes from the parts of Austin construction that are still growing: data centers, east-side industrial campuses, highway drainage and single-family subdivisions, rather than the collapsing apartment pipeline. It ranks first because it combines a legally fixed purchase calendar, owner-operator cash flow and a launch budget of roughly **$6,100–$9,600 [est.]**. Two independent research tracks, compliance and construction, also reached it separately. Its weakness is just as concrete. At least eight firms already sell SWPPP inspection in Austin, and no public per-inspection price was found, so the first 30 days of the plan below are a price-and-demand test with explicit kill criteria. Each runner-up beats SWPPP for a specific kind of founder:

- **Aerobic septic maintenance** has the strongest recurring mandate, but a licensing gate favors truck owners willing to apprentice.
- **Non-homestead property tax protest consulting** has 205,000 Travis protests a year behind it and only $275 in state fees.
- **Registered Accessibility Specialist (RAS)** work pays the most per license dollar, but it requires a degree.
- **VetHUB-certified government micro-contracting** is a scarcity window open only to service-disabled veterans.
- **Contractor bookkeeping** fits people who already have accounting skills.

Your skills, vehicle, veteran status and degree reorder this list, so the decision path near the end matters more than the ranking.

*How to read this report: claims with a linked source are sourced (several came from search-result text because many primary government sites, including TCEQ, TDLR, austintexas.gov and USAspending, were blocked during research). Anything tagged **[est.]** is the research team's arithmetic or judgment and has no source. **[unverified]** marks a figure that sources disagree on or that the researchers could not confirm.*

## Six picks survive a screen of more than 40 niches

The research covered five parts of the economy: public procurement, compliance-mandated services, construction and infrastructure, Texas licensing niches, and acquisition/B2B. Each candidate was tested on four things:

1. Does a law or contract set the buying calendar, so customers cannot simply defer?
2. Does it fit $10,000?
3. Can at least one type of founder enter without a multi-year experience gate?
4. Is there evidence that local supply isn't already saturated?

Only a handful passed on all four, and none passed decisively on the fourth, because license rosters and local price sheets were largely unreachable.

The cuts matter as much as the picks, because several "hidden" niches turn out to be crowded or capital-heavy.

**Backflow testing is confirmed crowded.** One Austin tester advertises **$75 per device** ([WaterCrest Backflow](https://watercrestbackflow.net/)), which confirms your earlier screen. It works as an add-on, not a standalone business.

**Grease-trap pumping is capital-heavy.** It is legally required every 90 days in Austin, but only a City-permitted liquid-waste hauler may remove the waste ([Austin Water](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/grease-trap-maintenance)). That means buying a vacuum truck. The airport's grease-trap contract drew **only two offers** for a $1.1M award ([City of Austin RCA 25-0742](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=451158)), which proves thin competition, but a contractor worker died after falling into an airport grease trap ([KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/investigations/city-notified-of-legal-claim-after-worker-died-falling-into-austin-airport-grease-trap/)).

- Cooling-tower inspection requires a professional engineer or a TDLR ACR Class A licensee ([Austin Water](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/cooling-tower-efficiency-program)).
- Elevator inspection requires QEI certification ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/elevator/elefaq.htm)).
- Sprinkler inspection requires NICET Level II ([TDI](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/fire/fmlisprinkler.html)).

**Other candidates are saturated or have no moat:**
- Kitchen hood cleaning needs no license but faces franchises like HOODZ and Kitchen Guard ([HOODZ Austin](https://www.hoodzinternational.com/austin/)).
- At least seven operators already market "zero-cost" micro-markets to Austin offices and apartments ([Austin Micro Market Vending](https://austinmicrovending.com/); [Nano Market ATX](https://www.nanomarketatx.com/blog)).
- Used shred trucks alone cost **$15K–$80K+** ([Crestmont Capital](https://www.crestmontcapital.com/blog/shredding-truck-financing)).
- Permit expediting is losing its reason to exist. The City is trying to cut site-plan review from **12–14 months to about six** ([City of Austin](https://www.austintexas.gov/development-services/expedited-site-plan-review)), and the megaprojects (Tesla, Samsung, most data centers) sit outside City jurisdiction ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/03/24/tesla-giga-texas-site-plan-ecological-paradise-terafab-north-campus-travis-county/)).
- Texas's 2025 cottage-food overhaul (SB 541) tripled the sales cap to $150K and banned local permit fees ([Homemade Texas](https://homemadetexas.org/sb-541-passes-effective-september-1-2025/)). That is real, but it creates no barrier against the next entrant.

Your earlier appliance-repair pick is still a sound skills-based baseline. This search deliberately looked for demand that the law guarantees instead.

## Stormwater inspection wins because the law sets the calendar

The core of the case is legal cadence. TCEQ's general permit TXR150000 runs from **March 2023 to March 2028**. It requires a documented inspection **at least every 14 calendar days plus within 24 hours of a 0.5-inch rain**, or every 7 days under the alternative schedule ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/stormwater/construction); [TCEQ 2023 CGP](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/stormwater/general/construction/2023-cgp-txr150000.pdf)). Records must be kept for three years after the site files its Notice of Termination ([Projexiv](https://projexiv.com/swppp-compliance-texas-construction-sites/)). The state requires only "qualified personnel" familiar with erosion controls. CPESC/CESSWI credentials are recommended but not mandated ([EcoPermitPros](https://www.ecopermitpros.com/post/tx-swp3-inspections-guide)). That puts SWPPP in a rare category: a service whose buying cadence is set by statute, with no state license to enter. Every other mandated inspection in the screen required a license, a degree or a truck.

At the 14-day cadence one active site generates **about 26 routine inspections a year plus rain events [est.]**. Sites run for months to years, so a book of 20–40 active sites becomes a weekly route with recurring monthly billing [est.].

**The demand lives where Austin construction is still growing.** As of March 2026 there were **more than 70 data-center projects** under construction between Temple and San Antonio, totaling about **5,600 MW** ([Austin American-Statesman](https://www.statesman.com/business/technology/article/data-center-central-texas-map-22091613.php)). Examples include:
- a 1.28M sq ft, 600 MW Skybox campus in Hutto ([Blackridge Research](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/blog/largest-biggest-upcoming-new-data-centers-in-texas-united-states));
- a 1,515-acre, 2 GW park in Caldwell County ([Blackridge Research](https://www.blackridgeresearch.com/blog/largest-biggest-upcoming-new-data-centers-in-texas-united-states)).

Other large sites are also in the pipeline:
- Tesla filed plans for about **5.2M sq ft** of new Giga Texas construction in March 2026 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/03/24/tesla-giga-texas-site-plan-ecological-paradise-terafab-north-campus-travis-county/)).
- The I-35 drainage tunnel segment runs from mid-2025 to 2029 ([MySA](https://www.mysanantonio.com/news/local/traffic/article/i35-austin-construction-projects-2026-21221633.php)).
- Kyle approved a **2,784-home, 532-acre** subdivision ([Houston Chronicle](https://www.chron.com/news/article/hays-county-homes-kyle-22319301.php)).

The apartment bust counts against most construction-linked businesses. Austin's 5+ unit permits fell **97% year-over-year to 41 in August 2025** ([Team Price](https://teamprice.com/articles/aug-2025-multifamily-permits-collapse)). SWPPP is less exposed because it follows disturbed acreage, not building type. Single-family permits were still **17.3% above** their historical March average while multifamily collapsed ([MyChesCo/Zillow](https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/national/zillow-warns-housing-pipeline-is-shrinking-as-permits-fall/)).

SWPPP also has the best cash profile of the construction candidates. Payroll businesses such as final cleaning, flagging and cabling crews must float wages through Texas payment chains. Owners have **35 days** to pay the prime contractor, primes have **7 days** to pay subcontractors, and **10% retainage** is standard ([Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/prompt-payment/texas-prompt-payment-faqs/)). The team estimated that floating a three-person crew for eight weeks costs about $21K, more than the whole budget [est.]. A solo inspector billing monthly per site carries almost no payroll float [est.]. Contractors also can't easily hire the people to do this in-house: **92% of U.S. construction firms** report difficulty hiring ([AGC, Aug 2025](https://www.agc.org/news/2025/08/28/construction-workforce-shortages-are-leading-cause-project-delays-immigration-enforcement-affects)).

The steelman against it is strong and deserves equal weight. At least eight firms already sell SWPPP services in Austin, some with 20+ years in business and certified staff:
- Red River Storm Water, ProjectCompli, Sure SWPPP, Smart SWPPP, TX Erosion Inspections, Compliance Resources, InstaSWPPP and Construction EcoServices ([Red River](https://www.redriverstormwater.com/); [ProjectCompli](https://www.projectcompli.com/austin-swppp/); [Smart SWPPP](https://www.smartswppp.com/markets/austin-swppp-services/); [Construction EcoServices](https://www.constructionecoservices.com/services/swppp/swppp-services-austin/)).

Smart SWPPP sells online ordering, a sign of commoditization. Every provider quotes on request, so the per-inspection price, which is the single number the whole business depends on, is **unknown**. Large GCs can legally have their own superintendents inspect. And the heavy-civil pipeline keeps slipping into 2027 and beyond ([KUT](https://www.kut.org/transportation/2026-07-24/austin-tx-project-connect-explainer-capmetro-light-rail-bus-train)). The realistic customer is therefore the **small and mid-size builder, land developer or site-work subcontractor** on the eastern and southern fringe (Hutto, Taylor, Kyle, Bastrop, Del Valle), not the hyperscale campus [est.]. SWPPP is ranked first because none of these objections has been confirmed, and each can be tested cheaply in 30 days.

## A 90-day launch costs about $6,100 to $9,600

The plan assumes you already own a reliable vehicle. A pickup helps on muddy sites but isn't required [est.]. It is built so that the cheapest disproof tests happen first, before most of the money is spent.

| PPE, camera, rain-alert weather service | $200–$400 | [est.] |

| Receivables reserve for 45–75 days of payment float | $2,500–$3,000 | Payment chain per [Levelset](https://www.levelset.com/prompt-payment/texas-prompt-payment-faqs/); float length [est.] |
| **Total** | **~$6,100–$9,600** | |

**Days 1–30: test price and demand before committing.**
- Request quotes from four or five incumbents while posing as a developer with a hypothetical 3-acre and a 15-acre site. This produces the price data no source published.
- Query TCEQ's Central Registry for active construction-permit authorizations in Travis, Williamson and Hays to gauge the pool of sites and whether it is growing.
- Confirm with the City of Austin and Travis County whether either requires a certified inspector locally. That was unresolved in the research.
- In parallel, form the LLC, complete training and get insurance quotes, but don't bind a policy until the price test passes.

**Days 31–60: win the first three to five paying sites.**
- Target small builders and site-work subcontractors on subdivision and industrial-pad work in Kyle and Hutto.
- Recruit erosion-control installers as referral partners: they install silt fence and rock entrances, you inspect.
- Offer a free compliance check of an existing site's SWPPP binder as the door-opener [est.].

**Days 61–90: reach 8–12 sites and organize them into a route.**
- Group sites geographically to cut drive time.
- Add plan-writing and termination-filing work as upsells.
- Register on the City's procurement portal and the state CMBL ($70/yr; [GovUnpacked](https://www.govunpacked.com/blog/texas-state-vendor-registration)) for public site work, but don't count on public revenue in year one.

For a sense of scale only: at an **assumed** $125 per inspection (not a sourced price), 20 sites would gross about $65K a year and 40 sites about $130K [est.].

**Kill criteria.** Stop or pivot if any of these hold:
- Incumbents quote below roughly **$75–100 per inspection** bundled with plan writing.
- Fewer than three paying sites after 50+ qualified contacts by day 90.
- Most prospects say their superintendents already inspect in-house [est. thresholds].

If you pivot, the insurance, route and builder relationships carry over to the flagging, final-clean or BMP-maintenance services the same customers buy ([TxDOT flagger rules via SafetyRegulatory](https://safetyregulatory.com/certifications/flagger-certification/); [Angi final-clean pricing](https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-clean-construction-site.htm)).

**Key risks beyond price** [est. unless cited]:
- **Liability:** a missed rain-event inspection exposes the permittee to TCEQ enforcement and you to an E&O claim, so a documented rain-alert system is not optional.
- **Concentration:** one developer can easily be 40% of an early book. The due-diligence norm of capping any single customer near 20–25% applies here too ([Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/small-business-due-diligence)).
- **Cyclicality:** Austin's residential permitting kept sliding into September 2026 ([The Real Deal, headline only](https://therealdeal.com/texas/2026/09/28/austins-residential-permitting-figures-slide-further/)). A data-center slowdown tied to power interconnection, which the research could not assess, would remove the strongest demand driver.

## The runners-up each hinge on a credential or asset you may already hold

### Aerobic septic maintenance has the hardest mandate and the hardest door

No candidate has a more inelastic mandate:
- Texas requires aerobic septic systems to be inspected **every four months** by a licensed maintenance provider under a contract required by Health & Safety Code §366.0515, and the provider must file a report with the county after each visit ([Texas Septic Guide](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/texas-septic-regulations)).
- Travis County Code Chapter 448 requires a continuous contract, with reports filed through the county portal ([Mac Septic](https://macseptic.com/aerobic-septic-permits-tceq-texas/)).
- Hays requires ongoing maintenance on all aerobic systems ([Hays County](https://www.hayscountytx.gov/septic-permits)).
- Williamson estimates that about **25% of its households** use on-site sewage systems ([Williamson County](https://www.wilcotx.gov/650/Property-Owner)).
- Contracts sell for about **$300–$500 a year** ([Texas Septic Guide](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/aerobic-septic-system-cost-texas)).
- An Austin-metro firm was advertising for maintenance providers, a sign of labor demand ([Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-aerobic-septic-jobs.html)).

The team estimated that 300 contracts would mean about 900 visits and **$90–120K a year** [est.]. The door is licensing. Holding contracts yourself requires one of three things: an Installer II license, a Class C wastewater-operator license, or **three years** as a registered maintenance provider or technician ([TCEQ](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/licensing/licenses/ossflic)). Another TCEQ snippet says there is no experience minimum, a conflict you must resolve by calling TCEQ [unverified]. Sources also disagree on whether Travis homeowners may maintain their own systems [unverified].

The realistic path runs through several steps [est.]:
1. Register as a maintenance technician (**$111**; [30 TAC §30.245](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/30-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-30-245)) and take the **$340** basic course ([Sure Nuff](https://surenuffseptictraining.com/basic-maintenance-provider-training-course/)).
2. Work under a licensed provider.
3. Pursue the Class C route or buy a retiring provider's contract book with seller financing.

It also pairs with SWPPP. Both are exurban compliance routes in the same Hays and Williamson geography [est.].

**Key risks:** the licensing timeline, lapsed-contract enforcement that turns out to be weak, and the need to own a pickup.

### Property tax consulting pays in the segment the national apps serve worst

Demand is set by law and is large:
- Travis owners and agents filed about **205,000 protests in 2025**, up from 181,509 in 2024 ([Austin Bulldog](https://theaustinbulldog.org/205000-property-value-protests-filed/)).
- Agents filed **90%** of them ([Austin Bulldog](https://theaustinbulldog.org/150000-property-value-protests-filed/)).
- Only **2,216 consultants** are registered statewide ([TDLR PTC at a Glance](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/PTC%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf)).
- Entry costs **$275 in TDLR fees**, 40 classroom hours and a PSI exam ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/forms/PTCapp.pdf)).

**The homeowner segment is a trap.** Pricing is compressing: Ownwell charges 25% against a typical 35–50% ([Ownwell](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/property-tax-protest-companies-cost)), and $50 flat-fee filings now target Travis ([TaxProtestTx](https://taxprotesttx.com/travis-county)). Voters raised the homestead exemption to **$140,000** and the business personal property exemption to **$125,000** ([Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/11/04/texas-property-tax-vote-constitutional-amendment/)), and TCAD reported a **1.8% average single-family decline** for 2026 ([TCAD, snippet](https://traviscad.org/news/2026-market-values-on-their-way)).

**The opening is non-homestead property:** 2–20 unit rentals, investor single-family homes, small commercial buildings and land. The 10% homestead cap doesn't limit those assessments, so a successful protest can save owners more. Williamson alone logged about **29,380 business-property protests** in 2024 ([vendor-run WCAD stats](https://williamsoncountypropertytaxtrends.com/tax-protests-filed/)). Your earlier screen found Travis only 53% owner-occupied, which implies a large pool of investor-owned homes outside the homestead cap [est.].

**Key risks:**
- Seasonality: clients sign up January–May, and contingency fees arrive months later.
- The ongoing automation of residential protests.

### RAS offers the best return per license dollar, if you have the degree

Texas projects of **$50,000 or more** must be registered with TDLR and receive RAS plan review and final inspection ([Access Plan Review](https://www.accessplanreview.com/)). There were **24,686 project registrations** statewide in FY24 ([TDLR EAB at a Glance](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/EAB%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf)). One RAS charges a flat **$800** per project under $3M, and the license costs **$300** ([TDLR fees](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfees.htm)). In 2019 TDLR published a flyer titled "Texas needs you to become a Registered Accessibility Specialist" ([TDLR](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/info/RAS%20recruitment%205-13-19.pdf)); it is dated, but it is a shortage signal. At 100 projects a year, revenue would be about $80K [est.].

The gate is a degree in architecture, engineering, interior design or landscape architecture, "or equivalent" ([Texas Access FAQ](https://www.texasaccess.com/information/texas_access_faq/)). For anyone who has one, RAS jumps to first place.

**Key risks:** a construction slowdown, and local RAS counts that the research could not pull.

### Veterans get a certification window; everyone else gets new procurement rules

Two 2025–26 legal shifts create an information edge.

**The local formal-bid threshold doubled.** SB 1173 raised it from $50K to **$100,000** for cities, counties, school districts and transit authorities, effective September 1, 2025. Cities must now contact at least two HUBs for purchases between **$3,000 and $100,000** ([TML](https://www.tml.org/1053/June-20-2025-Number-24)). More work is therefore bought by informal quotes sent to known vendors, so relationships with department buyers now matter more than watching bid boards [est.].

**The HUB pool collapsed to service-disabled veterans.** The Comptroller converted HUB into **VetHUB**, limited to veterans with a **≥20% service-connected disability** ([Comptroller](https://comptroller.texas.gov/about/media-center/news/20251202-acting-texas-comptroller-kelly-hancock-announces-emergency-rules-for-revamped-vethub-program-1764695317378)). That shrank the certified pool from **15,790** to about **1,513** by April 2026 ([KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/judge-temporarily-blocks-changes-to-the-historically-underutilized-business-program/)). State agencies must still contact two HUBs for $5K–$25K purchases ([HHSC](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/doing-business-with-hhs/contracting/hub/hub-procurement-thresholds.pdf)). A qualifying veteran who wraps any light service in VetHUB, CMBL and SAM registration becomes one of very few names buyers can call [est.]. That service could be SWPPP, office moves, document scanning or meeting AV.

**Key risks for this route:**
- A Travis County court temporarily restored the decertified HUBs in April 2026, and **trial is set for November 9, 2026** ([KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/judge-temporarily-blocks-changes-to-the-historically-underutilized-business-program/)), so the window could close.
- First government awards typically take **12–18 months** ([GT APEX](https://gtapexaccelerator.org/frequently-asked-questions/)), so public revenue can't carry year one. The Texas Prompt Payment Act's **30-day** terms ([Comptroller eXpendit](https://fmx.cpa.texas.gov/fm/pubs/purchase/prompt_pay/index.php)) help only once awards arrive.

### Contractor bookkeeping and commercial refrigeration are skill plays

U.S. accounting degrees fell to **55,152 in 2023–24**, a 20-year low ([Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/accountant-shortage)). Construction bookkeeping (job costing, work-in-progress schedules, pay applications) earns more than generic bookkeeping [est.], and it pairs with Austin's large contractor base and with seasonal tax-protest work. Local competition wasn't measured.

For trained technicians, commercial refrigeration and kitchen-equipment maintenance contracts are the B2B upgrade of your appliance-repair pick. The industry association CFESA describes a technician shortage ([CFESA](https://cfesa.com/)), and one Austin firm cites a customer who waited **24–48 hours** for competitors' quotes ([512 Refrigeration](https://512refrigeration.com/commercial-kitchen-equipment-repair/)). The Texas licensing needed for refrigeration work is **[unverified]**.

### Buying a business with $10K mostly buys a job

**SBA financing caps the deal size.** SBA rules effective June 2025 require a **10% equity injection**. A seller note can supply half of that only if it is on full standby, with no payments, for the life of the loan ([Whiteford](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending); [Reins](https://www.myreins.com/blog/sba-changes-sop-50-10-8-update)). So $10K supports at most a ~$100K SBA project with cash alone, or ~$200K with a standby seller note [est.]. From **October 1, 2026**, acquisition loans must be underwritten on historical cash flow, not projections ([MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/sba-stopped-lending-on-projections-sop-50-10-8-1-closes-acquisition-loans-on-last-year-s-numbers)).

**Seller financing is scarce and the listings are out of reach.** **90% of buyers** expect seller financing, but only **29% of owners** plan to offer it ([Sundance/BizBuySell Q2 2026](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)). The only B2B Austin listing with visible cash flow near this range, a snack-distribution route, asked **$135K with $58K down** for **$40,728** in net income ([BizBuySell](https://www.bizbuysell.com/texas/austin/owner-financed-businesses-for-sale/)).

Acquisition is therefore a year-two accelerant rather than a $10K entry. The most interesting version is buying a retiring septic provider's contract book once you are registered, though whether contracts and licenses transfer is **[unverified]**.

## Conclusion

In Austin in 2026 the best non-obvious openings are defined less by industry than by where law and licensing meet a supply the market can't quickly expand. The same rulebook explains both the picks and the cuts. Backflow testing and hood cleaning have mandates but no entry barrier, so prices fall to the floor. Cooling-tower and elevator inspection have barriers so high that a newcomer can't get in. The winners sit in between: SWPPP's barrier is know-how and relationships, septic's is a license you can apprentice into, RAS's is a degree, and VetHUB's is status. That means "which business" is really "which barrier do you already stand on the right side of." The decision path is therefore the most important output, and SWPPP ranks first mostly because it asks the least of the founder.

The 2025–26 policy churn is itself an opportunity for someone who reads the fine print. The $100K informal-bid band, the VetHUB conversion, the new SBA underwriting rules effective three days from now, and the homestead and BPP exemption hikes all quietly reshuffled who can win which work. Incumbents built their pipelines around the old rules. The biggest remaining unknowns are local provider counts and local prices, and a founder can close both in the first 30 days with phone calls and one registry query before spending most of the $10K.
