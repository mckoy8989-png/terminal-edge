# Texas Licensing, Legal and Regulatory Niches for Austin-Metro Businesses Under $10K

Research date: 2026-09-28. Labels: **SOURCED** means the figure comes from the linked page. **ESTIMATE** means my own inference or arithmetic. Several primary domains were blocked by the egress proxy (traviscad.org, tdlr.texas.gov, theaustinbulldog.org), so some TCAD, TDLR and Austin Bulldog figures come from search-engine snippets of those pages rather than full-page reads. ## Q1. Property tax protest consulting (TCAD / WCAD): demand, pay model, TDLR registration, concentration, room for niche entrants

### Takeaway
Demand is large and set by law. Travis County saw about 205,000 protests in 2025, up from 181,509 in 2024, and Williamson about 85,000 in 2024. Agents already file about 90% of Travis protests, and pricing is falling toward 25–30% contingency or flat $30–$50 fees. Registration is cheap: $275 in TDLR fees plus 40 hours of education and a PSI exam. The generic homeowner segment, however, is crowded and being automated. Three changes in 2025–2026 cut into residential economics: the homestead exemption rose to $140K, the business personal property (BPP) exemption rose to $125K, and TCAD reported flat-to-falling 2026 home values. A newcomer's realistic opening is non-homestead niches such as small commercial, 2–20 unit rentals and land, where the 10% homestead cap does not apply and national apps do less well.

### Cited Findings
**Demand (volumes)**
- SOURCED (2025, TCAD chief appraiser Leana Mann via Austin Bulldog): Travis County property owners and agents filed about **205,000 protests in 2025**, which is 23,360 more than the **181,509 filed in 2024**. The total grew from about 150,000 two days before the May 15 deadline. Snippet only; the page was blocked. — [Austin Bulldog, "205,000 property value protests filed"](https://theaustinbulldog.org/205000-property-value-protests-filed/)
- SOURCED (May 2025, same outlet): At about 150,000 filings, "Agents filed **90 percent** of the protests on behalf of the property owners they represent and individual owners filed the rest." Nearly **72%** were filed electronically. TCAD aimed to certify rolls by July 18. — [Austin Bulldog, "150,000 property value protests filed"](https://theaustinbulldog.org/150000-property-value-protests-filed/)
- Conflicting figure: a vendor blog says Travis had a "record **210,000** protest filings — 90% of them residential" in 2025. That conflicts with the 205,000 above. Prefer 205,000 (chief appraiser via the press). — [Home Tax Shield Travis guide 2026](https://hometaxshield.com/posts/travis-county-property-tax-protest-guide-2026/)
- SOURCED (early April 2026, TCAD via Austin American-Statesman snippet): about **60,000 protests** had been filed by early April 2026, before the May 15, 2026 deadline. I found no final 2026 total. — [Statesman](https://www.statesman.com/news/article/texas-property-tax-protest-deadline-homeowners-22194232.php)
- SOURCED (2026, TCAD release via search snippet): the 2026 median residential homestead value was **$493,449**, and single-family homes saw an average **1.8% decline**. — [TCAD news, "2026 Market Values On Their Way"](https://traviscad.org/news/2026-market-values-on-their-way) (snippet; page blocked)
- SOURCED (2024, vendor-run stats site, likely built on WCAD data): Williamson CAD had **85,030 protests in 2024**, about **31% of parcels**. These were **55,660 residential** and **29,380 business real estate** (27,440 business in 2023). Owners saved about **$82M** in 2024. **54%** got reductions at the informal stage and **55%** at the ARB. Note that 55,660 + 29,380 = 85,040, so the breakdown is slightly inconsistent. — [williamsoncountypropertytaxtrends.com](https://williamsoncountypropertytaxtrends.com/tax-protests-filed/)
- Gap: I found no official WCAD 2025 or 2026 protest count ([WCAD KB](https://support.wcad.org/portal/en/kb/williamson-central-appraisal-district/protest) has only the process).

**How consultants get paid**
- SOURCED (2026, Ownwell's own blog): "Most property tax protest firms in Texas charge **35% to 50%** of first-year savings." Ownwell says it charges a flat **25%** and nothing if there is no savings. It puts O'Connor at about **50%**, Texas Protax and Five Stone at about **40%**, and Home Tax Shield at **$30/yr + 30%**. All of these are competitor claims. — [Ownwell cost breakdown 2026](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/property-tax-protest-companies-cost)
- SOURCED (2026, listing titles): DIY and flat-fee products now target Travis, e.g. "Travis County Property Tax Protest 2026 — File for $50." This is evidence of price compression. — [TaxProtestTx](https://taxprotesttx.com/travis-county); [appealdesk comparisons](https://www.appealdesk.com/compare/best-texas-property-tax-protest-services)
- SOURCED: O'Connor has operated since 1974 and handles complex commercial work and litigation. — [Ownwell comparison](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/property-tax-protest-companies-cost); O'Connor calls itself the largest US protest firm by volume, a self-claim — [Resolute summary](https://resolutepts.com/best-property-tax-protest-companies-in-texas/)
- SOURCED (law): The owner appoints an agent on the Comptroller's form under **Tax Code §1.111** (Form 50-162), signed by the owner. — [Tax Code §1.111](https://texas.public.law/statutes/tex._tax_code_section_1.111). Consultant registration is governed by **Occupations Code ch. 1152**. — [OC 1152](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/OC/htm/OC.1152.htm)

**TDLR Property Tax Consultant registration**
- SOURCED (TDLR application form, via snippet): you need **40 classroom hours**: 8 on laws and rules, 16 on appraisal and valuation, 8 on property tax consulting and 8 on ethics. You must also pass the **PSI Property Tax Consultant exam**, with the fee paid to PSI and listed in the Candidate Information Bulletin. — [TDLR PTC application](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/forms/PTCapp.pdf); [TDLR exam page](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/taxprof/taxprofexam.htm)
- SOURCED: fees are a **$50 application fee + $225 registration fee = $275**. — [TDLR PTC application](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/forms/PTCapp.pdf)
- SOURCED: renewal requires **24 CE hours** (8 law, 4 ethics, 6 appraisal, 6 consulting). — [TDLR PTC CE](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcce.htm)
- SOURCED: the Comptroller and TAPTP are among the approved education providers. — [TDLR pre-license education list](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ptc/ptcPreLicEduList.htm); [TAPTP](https://taptp.org/education-events/)
- SOURCED (FY2024, TDLR "PTC at a Glance," snippet): **2,216 property tax consultant licensees statewide.** — [TDLR PTC at a Glance](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/PTC%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf)

**Policy headwinds (2025–2026)**
- SOURCED (Nov 4, 2025): voters approved raising the school homestead exemption to **$140,000** (Prop 13, retroactive to TY2025). They also raised the BPP exemption from **$2,500 to $125,000** (Prop 9). — [Texas Tribune](https://www.texastribune.org/2025/11/04/texas-property-tax-vote-constitutional-amendment/); [Ownwell summary](https://www.ownwell.com/blog/texas-proposition-13-homestead-exemption)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, market size: take about 205K Travis protests, 90% agent-filed, so about 185K agent-represented accounts. Assume average savings of $500–$1,000 and a 25–40% fee, giving roughly $125–$375 of fee per account. That implies about **$23M–$70M/yr in Travis agent fees**, plus WCAD, Hays and Bastrop. This is an order-of-magnitude figure only, because savings per account are not published.
- ESTIMATE, concentration: 90% agent share against only 2,216 registrants statewide suggests volume is concentrated in a few high-throughput firms (O'Connor, Ownwell, Texas Protax, Five Stone, and others). The tail of small consultants lives on referrals. I found no published market-share data for Travis.
- ESTIMATE, the best niche for a new entrant is **non-homestead property**: small commercial, strip retail, 2–20 unit rentals, STR and investor single-family homes, and land. Reasons:
  - The 10% homestead cap and the $140K exemption shrink residential savings.
  - Falling 2026 Austin values make it easier to win reductions but shrink absolute dollars.
  - WCAD alone had about 29K business-property protests in 2024.
  - The **$125K BPP exemption removes most small-business BPP work**, which counts against a "BPP rendition" niche.
- ESTIMATE, startup cost is about **$1.5K–$4K**. That covers a course (about $500–$1,000, not verified), the TDLR $275, the PSI exam, a comps data subscription such as MLS or a paid comps service, E&O insurance, an LLC and a basic site. It fits under $10K.
- ESTIMATE, cash flow risk: contingency fees arrive only after ARB settlement (June–Aug) and tax bills (Oct–Jan). Year-1 revenue is lumpy, and client acquisition in Jan–May is the main cost.
- **What would disprove it**:
  - (a) 2026 final TCAD protest counts fall sharply below 180K, or the agent share falls because of DIY or AI tools.
  - (b) Evidence that national firms already win most small commercial and rental accounts in Travis/WCAD at ≤25%.
  - (c) TCAD/WCAD informal settlement rates for non-homestead property below about 40%.
  - (d) The PSI exam pass rate or the course cost turning out to be far higher than assumed.

### Gaps
- PSI exam fee and pass rate, course prices, and whether any supervised-experience rule applies. The TDLR pages were blocked, so I could not confirm "Senior" vs basic registration tiers.
- Official WCAD 2025/2026 protest totals, Hays and Bastrop totals, and the final 2026 TCAD total.
- Average savings per protested account by property class in Travis. This is needed to size fee revenue.
- Any 2025 legislative change to agent fee rules or ARB procedures, which I did not research.

## Q2. Agricultural / wildlife (1-d-1) valuation services for small-acreage owners in Austin's ring (Hays, Bastrop, western Travis)

### Takeaway
Texas 1-d-1 open-space valuation can cut taxable land value by 90% or more. Hays CAD publishes concrete minimums: bees on 5+ acres, wildlife management on 14.29+ acres (12.5 in a co-op, 11.11 for endangered target species). Wildlife plans must follow a prior ag use history. There is a clear service niche in preparing PWD-885 wildlife plans, keeping annual activity logs and managing hives or grazing leases, and it needs no state license. Pricing ranges widely ($149 online templates to $2,000–$5,000 for biologist plans), so templated competition is already present at the low end.

### Cited Findings
- SOURCED (HaysCAD guidelines; the PDF is dated 2019, so check the current version): **beekeeping** needs a minimum of **5 acres plus homestead acreage**, apiary registration with the Texas Apiary Inspection Service, and a forage landscape plan. Hives must be on site **9 months/yr**. — [HaysCAD 1-d-1 guidelines](https://hayscad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/HAYSAG-GUIDELINES-STANDARDS-2019.pdf)
- SOURCED (same): the **wildlife** minimum is **14.29 acres**, or **12.50 acres per member** in a wildlife property association, or **11.11 acres** if the target species is candidate, threatened or endangered. You "must start with traditional 1d1 Agricultural Use" before converting to wildlife use. — [HaysCAD guidelines](https://hayscad.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/HAYSAG-GUIDELINES-STANDARDS-2019.pdf)
- SOURCED: wildlife plans use TPWD form **PWD-885**. — [TPWD PWD-885](https://tpwd.texas.gov/publications/pwdforms/media/pwd_885_w7000_open_space_agric_valuation_wildlife_mgmt_plan.pdf)
- SOURCED (vendor, 2026): a biologist-prepared plan costs about **$2,000–$5,000**. Low-end providers start at **$300**, and online plans cost **$149** statewide with PWD-885 prep. — [Thorpe Land Services](https://thorpeland.com/wildlife/cost); [Hill Country Land & Wildlife](https://www.hillcountrylandwildlife.com/wildlife-management-plans/)
- SOURCED (vendor): 1-d-1 valuation "can reduce the taxable value of a property by 90% or more." One vendor says a 250-acre ranch might pay up to $5,000/yr without the valuation and under $200 with it. — [Texas Land Tax](https://texaslandtax.com/blog/wildlife-management-plan-texas-property-tax/); [Hill Country Land & Wildlife services](https://www.hillcountrylandwildlife.com/wildlife-management-services/)
- SOURCED: beekeeping-for-ag-valuation hive management is an existing Central Texas service category (e.g. PachaMama Bees, Bee Mindful). — [PachaMama Bees](https://www.pachamamabees.com/agricultural-exemption); [Bee Mindful](https://www.bee-mindful.com/ag-exemptions); [Texas Beekeepers Assn](https://texasbeekeepers.org/ag-valuation/)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, the best business model is recurring rather than one-off. Charge for the plan (about $300–$1,500), then an annual compliance retainer: activity log, photos, the CAD annual report where required, and handling CAD audit letters. For bees, add hive placement and maintenance, about $X per hive per year (no price found). Owners of 5–20 acre exurban lots on Austin's west and southwest edge (Dripping Springs, Wimberley, Spicewood, Bastrop) are the target.
- ESTIMATE, startup cost: $1K–$8K. The bee route needs hives, bees, protective gear, a truck and liability insurance. Plan writing needs little more than software and insurance. It fits under $10K.
- A property tax consultant registration (Q1) could be needed if you represent owners before the CAD or ARB for pay. Plan writing alone likely does not need it (ESTIMATE; check OC 1152).
- **What would disprove it**:
  - (a) HaysCAD, BCAD or TCAD data showing few new small-acreage 1-d-1 applications, or high denial rates.
  - (b) Evidence that $149 online plans satisfy CADs and have taken the market.
  - (c) CAD rules tightening, e.g. a higher bee acreage minimum or stricter 5-of-7-year history checks.
  - (d) Subdivision rules or HOAs blocking ag use on 5–15 acre lots.

### Gaps
- Counts of 1-d-1 parcels and new applications by county (Hays, Bastrop, Travis, Williamson). I did not find them, but they should be in CAD annual reports or the Comptroller's Property Value Study.
- Current (2025–2026) HaysCAD, BCAD and TCAD guidelines. The cited HaysCAD PDF is from 2019.
- Market pricing for annual bee-management retainers.

## Q3. Short/cheap Texas occupational licenses relative to earning power (TDLR, JBCC, DPS)

### Takeaway
Several state credentials cost only a few hundred dollars. For RAS, the barrier is the degree requirement, not cost. The work is mandatory by statute: every Texas project of $50K or more needs TDLR registration plus RAS plan review and inspection. JBCC process server certification is the cheapest and fastest entry at about $300–$400 all-in, but the service is commoditized. Mold assessment technician needs a 24-hour course and a $60 exam. A Texas PI license is cheap in fees but gated by 3 years of experience or a criminal justice degree, plus company licensing.

### Cited Findings
**Registered Accessibility Specialist (RAS), TDLR / Architectural Barriers Act**
- SOURCED: projects with an estimated cost of **$50,000 or more** must be registered with TDLR, with construction documents reviewed by a RAS. The TDLR project filing fee is **$175**. — [accessplanreview.com](https://www.accessplanreview.com/tas-inspection/)
- SOURCED: RAS application **$300**, renewal **$250**. The candidate needs a degree in architecture, engineering, interior design or landscape architecture, **or equivalent**. — [Texas Access FAQ](https://www.texasaccess.com/information/texas_access_faq/); [TDLR EAB at a Glance](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/EAB%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf)
- SOURCED (vendor price): one RAS charges a **flat $800** for plan review plus inspection on projects under $3M, statewide with no travel charge. — [accessplanreview.com](https://www.accessplanreview.com/tas-inspection/)

**Process server (JBCC, Texas Judicial Branch Certification Commission)**
- SOURCED: you need a **7-hour** JBCC-approved course taken within 1 year before applying. Certification costs **$200 for 2 years** and requires DPS/FBI fingerprinting and online application only. — [txcourts.gov JBCC requirements & fees](https://www.txcourts.gov/jbcc/process-server-certification/initial-certification/certification-requirements-fees/)
- SOURCED (vendor): the course costs about **$75–$150**. — [Noble Notary guide](https://legaldocprepnotary.com/how-to-become-a-process-server-in-texas/); [ServeNow TX](https://www.serve-now.com/resources/become-process-server/texas)

**Mold assessment technician (TDLR)**
- SOURCED: requirements are age 18+, a HS diploma or GED, a background check, a **24-hour** accredited course, a **$60 exam** and insurance per 16 TAC §78.40. A Texas office is required, either your own or your employer's. The Mold Assessment *Consultant* license fee is **$500**. — [TDLR mold technician application](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/forms/mld-lic-003-mold-assessment-technician-initial-license-application.pdf); [TDLR mold exam](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldexam.htm); [TradeCareerPath](https://tradecareerpath.com/trades/mold-inspector/texas/)

**Private investigator (DPS Private Security Program)**
- SOURCED: you need **3 consecutive years** of investigation experience, **or** a criminal justice bachelor's degree, **or** another bachelor's plus 12 months of experience, **or** a CJ associate degree plus 24 months. — [DPS company rep experience](https://www.dps.texas.gov/section/private-security/company-representative-experience-and-examination-information)
- SOURCED (aggregator, less reliable): initial fees are about **$135** plus 6 hours of training. — [LicenseMap / aggregators](https://getlicensemap.com/states/private-investigator/texas). DPS fee schedule not verified.

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, **RAS has the best earnings-to-license-cost ratio** of the group.
  - Demand is statutory and tied to Austin's construction volume. At about $800–$1,500 per project, 100 projects/yr would bring in about $80K–$150K.
  - The catch is eligibility: the degree or "equivalent" requirement plus TDLR training and exam. For someone without a design degree, this is not a quick path.
  - **What would disprove it**: TDLR data showing many RAS registrants per project in Central Texas, or price competition below about $600.
- ESTIMATE, **process serving** is cheap to enter (under $1K, including E&O insurance). It is a low-margin volume business: servers are paid per serve, often $50–$150 in Austin (unverified). It works as a feeder for PI or legal-support work.
  - **What would disprove it**: evidence that Travis County law firms route most serves through statewide platforms at below-cost rates.
- ESTIMATE, **mold assessment** fits under $10K: course, exam, license, insurance, and a moisture meter plus sampling kit. Austin's humidity and flood events (e.g. the July 2025 Hill Country floods) support demand. Two limits apply: a technician can only assess residential units within limits, and a Texas conflict-of-interest rule bars the same firm from both assessing and remediating a job.
  - **What would disprove it**: few Austin mold assessment searches or leads, or a saturated TDLR licensee count in Travis.
- ESTIMATE, **PI** is only viable for people who already have qualifying experience or a degree.

### Gaps
- Not researched due to tool budget: irrigator/installer (TCEQ, not TDLR), auctioneer, driver education, court reporting (JBCC), notary + loan signing, and code/building inspector (ICC certs). The report writer should treat these as unresearched, not as rejected.
- Counts of RAS, mold and process server licensees in the Austin metro, from TDLR/JBCC license search tools (blocked).
- The current TDLR mold technician license fee and insurance minimums.

## Q4. Other Texas-specific niches: cottage food, HOA-related services, probate/title/mineral

### Takeaway
Texas's 2025 cottage food overhaul (SB 541, effective Sept 1, 2025) tripled the sales cap to $150K, allowed some refrigerated foods and wholesale through vendors, and banned local permits and fees. That makes home food businesses in Austin unusually cheap to start. HOA management needs **no state license** in Texas, and Texas has over 21,000 HOAs. Recurring statutory touchpoints give a small operator billable work: resale certificates capped at $375, management certificates filed with TREC, and Chapter 209 notice rules. I found no usable evidence on probate cleanout or mineral/title abstracting in the time available.

### Cited Findings
**Cottage food**
- SOURCED: **SB 541**, effective **Sept 1, 2025**, raised the gross annual sales cap from **$50K to $150K**, indexed to inflation. It allows most foods except meat, poultry, seafood, ice, low-acid canned goods, CBD/THC and raw milk products. It permits refrigerated (TCS) foods direct-to-consumer and wholesale of non-TCS goods through registered cottage food vendors, and **prohibits local permits, licenses or fees**. — [Homemade Texas](https://homemadetexas.org/sb-541-passes-effective-september-1-2025/); [Farm and Ranch Freedom Alliance](https://farmandranchfreedom.org/cottage-food-law-sb541/); [Texas Cottage Food Law site](https://texascottagefoodlaw.com/sb541/)

**HOA services**
- SOURCED: "Texas does not require a specific HOA management licence." TREC keeps only a management-certificate database and has no enforcement jurisdiction over HOAs. — [TREC on HOA certificates](https://www.trec.texas.gov/article/trec%E2%80%99s-role-hoa-management-certificates); [CMGT Texas HOA laws](https://cmgt.org/resources/hoa-laws/texas)
- SOURCED: SB 711 (2025) expanded the management-certificate disclosures (management company contact details, document website, transfer fees). — [CompareHOAManagers / CMGT](https://cmgt.org/resources/hoa-laws/texas)
- SOURCED: the resale certificate fee is capped at **$375**, and an updated certificate at **$75**. — [communitypay.us Texas resale certificates](https://www.communitypay.us/resale-certificates/texas/)
- SOURCED (Community Associations Institute via aggregator): Texas has **more than 21,000 HOAs** covering **about 2.09M homes**, with **$9.5B/yr** in assessments. — [Lifetime HOA Management stats](https://lifetimehoamanagement.com/texas-hoa-statistics/)
- Governing law is Property Code **Chapter 209**. — [Property Code ch. 209](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/docs/PR/htm/PR.209.htm)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, **cottage food**: startup is about $500–$3K (food handler card, labels, equipment, farmers-market fees). Local permits are now banned, and wholesale through vendors is allowed. This is a legal-structure opportunity rather than a licensing moat, so expect many new entrants after 2025 and low barriers for competitors.
  - **What would disprove it**: Austin-area farmers markets saturated with cottage vendors, or city/county enforcement disputes over the local-fee ban.
- ESTIMATE, **self-managed small HOAs and deed-restriction inspection**: many small or older Austin-area HOAs (under 100 lots) are self-managed. No license is required, so a solo operator can offer:
  - drive-by deed-restriction inspections with violation letters that follow §209.006 notice rules,
  - TREC management-certificate filing,
  - resale-certificate preparation,
  - annual-meeting logistics.

  Startup is under $5K (E&O/fidelity bond, software). Large HOA management firms dominate master-planned communities.
  - **What would disprove it**: evidence that nearly all Travis/Williamson HOAs are already professionally managed, or that small HOAs won't pay about $300–$1,000/month.
- ESTIMATE: no licensing moat exists for HOA management, so competing on compliance knowledge of Ch. 209 and the 2025 SB 711 rules is the differentiator.

### Gaps
- Number of HOAs in Travis/Williamson and the share that are self-managed. TREC's database could be queried, but I did not do it.
- Probate/estate cleanout tied to Travis Probate Court No. 1 (filing volumes, Texas muniment of title and small-estate affidavit processes). Not researched; no sourced findings.
- Mineral/royalty or title abstracting in the Austin metro. Not researched. It is probably weak locally because Travis/Hays/Williamson are not major mineral counties (ESTIMATE, unverified).
- Notary/loan-signing economics in Austin. Not researched.
