## A1. Silver tsunami: how many owners are 55+ and how many lack succession plans (national, Texas, Austin)?

### Takeaway
About half of U.S. business owners are 55 or older, and a large minority have no workable exit plan. That produces a real flow of businesses for sale. I found no Texas- or Austin-specific owner-age figure; the local evidence is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings

Verify before quoting as final.)
- SOURCED: Project Equity counts about 2.9 million employer businesses with owners over 55. They support 32.1M employees, $1.3T in payroll and $6.5T in revenue. Project Equity puts the number of baby-boomer-owned businesses expected to transition at 2.3M, which covers about 1 in 6 private-sector jobs. — [Project Equity: Silver Tsunami](https://project-equity.org/impact/silver-tsunami/); [Project Equity press release](https://project-equity.org/press-releases/2-3-million-small-businesses-nationwide-owned-by-aging-boomers-preparing-to-retire-puts-1-in-6-employees-jobs-at-risk-based-on-a-project-equity-study/)

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE: Supply of retiring-owner businesses is real, but so is buyer competition. Texas is a top-five buyer-demand state, and 78% of buyers plan to use SBA loans (see A2). Tiny, owner-dependent firms (under $50K SDE) are the ones most likely to close unsold. That is the segment a $10K buyer can target, but these firms are also the least bankable.
- ESTIMATE: The "silver tsunami" helps a $10K buyer mainly by making sellers more willing to carry notes. Brokers are not discounting prices: multiples rose to 2.7x in Q2 2026.

## A2. Financing with little cash: SBA equity injection, seller notes, earn-outs. What can $10K buy?

### Takeaway
Under current SBA rules, a buyer must inject 10% of total project cost. A seller note can cover at most half of that, and only if it is on full standby for the life of the loan. So $10K of your own cash tops out at a total SBA project of about $200K, and realistically about $80–170K of purchase price. The practical sweet spot for $10K is a sub-$100K business bought mostly on seller financing.

### Cited Findings
**SBA SOP 50 10 8 (effective June 1, 2025)**
- SOURCED: The minimum equity injection is a hard 10% of total project costs for start-ups and complete changes of ownership. This reversed the 2023 rule that left the amount to lender discretion. — [Whiteford law client alert](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending); [Starfield & Smith, May 2025](https://starfieldsmith.com/2025/05/best-practices-a-review-of-equity-injection-requirements-under-sop-50-10-8/); [Live Oak Bank](https://www.liveoak.bank/blog/navigating-the-sbas-new-sop-50-10-8)
- SOURCED: A seller note counts toward the injection only if it is on full standby (no principal and no interest) for the entire loan term, typically 10 years. Even then it can cover no more than 50% of the required injection, which is 5% of the project. The old 24-month standby rule is obsolete. A second seller note on two-year limited standby can sit outside the equity calculation. — [Reins: SOP 50 10 8 seller notes](https://www.myreins.com/blog/sba-changes-sop-50-10-8-update); [Regalis Capital 2026 guide](https://learn.regaliscapital.com/guides/seller-financing-seller-notes-new-sba-rules/); [Clearly Acquired](https://www.clearlyacquired.com/blog/major-sop-50-10-8-sba-changes-effective-june-1-2025----what-they-mean-for-business-buyers)
- CONFLICT: Some pages still describe the older rule, where seller debt counts if it is on 24-month full standby or on interest-only partial standby with at least 25% of the injection from another source ([Pioneer Capital Advisory](https://www.pioneercapitaladvisory.com/post/equity-injection-explained-what-buyers-need-for-sba-approval)). That describes pre-June-2025 or legacy practice. The SOP 50 10 8 sources above supersede it.
- SOURCED: Partial changes of ownership must be structured as stock purchases, and all equity holders, including the remaining seller, must guarantee for at least 2 years. — [Pioneer/aggregated search summary; Whiteford](https://www.whitefordlaw.com/news-events/client-alert-sba-issues-sop-50-10-8-key-changes-impacting-sba-7a-lending)

**SOP 50 10 8.1 (effective October 1, 2026, three days after this research date)**
- SOURCED: SOP 50 10 8.1 replaces 50 10 8 on Oct 1, 2026. It sorts business purchases into four types, bans projections from the debt-coverage test (acquisition loans close on historical numbers), and cuts the personal financial statement (Form 413) window to 90 days. Investor equity used for the injection limits that investor's distributions to tax obligations until payoff. Equity from non-controlling minority investors can cover no more than half the injection, and it shares that half with any seller standby note. A standby-debt provider may not also take equity. — [StatementsReady](https://statementsready.com/blog/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-changes); [Jaken Finance Group](https://jakenfinancegroup.com/blog/sba-sop-50-10-8-1-october-2026-changes/); [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/sba-stopped-lending-on-projections-sop-50-10-8-1-closes-acquisition-loans-on-last-year-s-numbers); [SBA info notice 5000-868665](https://www.sba.gov/document/information-notice-5000-868665-issuance-sop-50-10-8-technical-updates)

**7(a) Small Loans (≤$350K)**
- SOURCED: SBA sunset the FICO SBSS minimum score (previously 165) for 7(a) Small Loans, effective March 1, 2026. It added a hard minimum 1.1x debt service coverage ratio. — [SBA procedural notice 5000-875701](https://legacy.sba.gov/document/procedural-notice-5000-875701-sunset-sbss-score-7a-small-loans); [FastWaySBA](https://www.fastwaysba.com/blog/sba-eliminates-sbss-requirement-for-small-7-a-loans-what-it-means-for-sba-approvals-in-2026)
- SOURCED: FY2026 guaranty fee, charged on the guaranteed portion, is reported as 2% for loans ≤$150K, 3% up to $700K and 3.5% up to $5M. SBA guarantees 85% of loans ≤$150K. — [FastWaySBA fees 2026](https://www.fastwaysba.com/blog/understanding-sba-loan-fees-2026); [NAGGL FY2026 fees](https://www.naggl.org/fy-2026-loan-fees-and-clarification-of-fee-calculation-for-multiple-wcp-or-ewcp-loans/). These are secondary summaries; confirm on the SBA fee calculator.
- SOURCED: In the BizBuySell Q2 2026 survey, 78% of buyers expect to use SBA financing. Brokers name the 10% injection / full-standby rule and the March 2026 SBA citizenship-rule update as top deal obstacles. — [Sundance Financial](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report)

**Seller financing norms**
- SOURCED: 90% of buyers expect seller financing, but only 29% of owners plan to offer it (BizBuySell, Q2 2026 survey). Pepperdine found 48% of 2023 sales involved a seller note. — [Sundance Financial](https://sundancefg.com/resources/bizbuysell-q2-2026-insight-report); [The Deal Sheet](https://thedealsheet.co/guides/seller-financing)
- SOURCED: IBBA data show seller financing in 75–90% of deals under $5M. In Q1 2026, seller financing covered about 10–16% of deal value in Main Street segments. Seller notes typically run 3–7 years at about 6–9% interest (Morgan & Westfield data center on 6–8%). — [The Deal Sheet 2026](https://thedealsheet.co/guides/seller-financing); [Iconic](https://iconic.co/blog/seller-financing-a-complete-guide/). These are aggregator figures and the underlying IBBA/Pepperdine reports were not read directly.
- Earn-outs: I found no small-deal-specific statistics on earn-out frequency. See Gaps.

### Inferences (ESTIMATE, worked math)
- **SBA, all-buyer-cash case:** 10% injection with $10K cash gives a total project of at most $100K. Project cost includes the purchase price, the SBA guaranty fee (about 1.7% of the loan at 2% of the 85% guaranteed portion), closing and legal costs, and any working capital. That implies a purchase price of roughly $80–90K.
- **SBA, max seller-standby case:** $10K buyer cash (5%) plus a seller note on full 10-year standby (5%) supports a total project of at most $200K, or roughly $170–185K purchase price after fees and working capital. The seller must accept zero payments on that note for about 10 years, and few will.
- **Coverage constraint:** Take a $150K SBA loan at about 10-year amortization and about 10–11% prime-plus rate (the rate is an ESTIMATE, not sourced). Annual debt service is about $24–25K. At a 1.1x minimum DSCR, and lenders usually want more, the business needs about $27K+ of cash available for debt service after the buyer's own living wage. From Oct 1, 2026 that must be shown in historical numbers.
- **Lender appetite:** Many SBA lenders avoid sub-$150K acquisition loans because fixed underwriting costs are high. Expect friction; this is an ESTIMATE, and SBA Express or Small-Loan-focused lenders and CDFIs are the likely channel.
- **Non-SBA seller-financed route (most realistic for $10K):** With $10K down at 15–25% down, the price is about $40–65K. A seller carries the rest over 3–5 years at 6–9%. At the 1.5–2.5x SDE multiples typical of small routes and service firms, that buys roughly $20–40K of SDE. That is a part-time or side income, not a full salary.

### Gaps
- The primary SBA SOP 50 10 8 / 8.1 PDFs were not read. Section numbers and exact text are unverified.
- No sourced data on how often earn-outs appear in sub-$500K Main Street deals.
- No sourced current SBA 7(a) interest rate figure.

## A4. Risks, scams and due diligence

### Takeaway
The main ways a small-deal buyer loses money are inflated SDE add-backs, customer concentration, and fake or unverifiable route revenue. Vending is the category most associated with "guaranteed income" scams. The main defenses are tax returns, bank statements, telemetry data, location contracts, and a seller note, which keeps the seller financially tied to the business's performance.

### Cited Findings
- SOURCED: Buy-side quality-of-earnings (QoE) reviews commonly disallow 10–30% of seller-proposed add-backs. At 4x, every $50K disallowed removes $200K of price. A broker's add-back list "has not been tested by anyone". — [Papermark 2026](https://www.papermark.com/blog/small-business-due-diligence)
- SOURCED: Customer concentration is the most common repricing trigger. A single customer at 20–25% or more of revenue is a material underwriting risk. — [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/small-business-due-diligence); [AcquireCalc](https://www.acquirecalc.com/guides/due-diligence-checklist)
- SOURCED: QoE costs about $15–25K on smaller deals and $35–60K on a $5M deal. — [Papermark](https://www.papermark.com/blog/small-business-due-diligence); [Commercial Capital](https://comcapfinancial.com/articles/quality-of-earnings-report/). ESTIMATE: that is disproportionate for a sub-$100K deal. A CPA review of tax returns against bank deposits, for about $1–3K, is the proportionate substitute.
- SOURCED: Vending red flags: "guaranteed income" promises such as "$1,000+/week minimum"; out-of-state "bulk deals" requiring a wire transfer; pricing far below plausible earnings (e.g., a machine said to make $600/week sold for $2,500); sellers who hide price or location or post only vague photos. Required verification: 12–24 months of tax returns, bank deposits, machine telemetry, and walking away from "napkin" records. — [TOTL Vending](https://www.totlvending.com/post/avoid-these-common-vending-machine-scams); [VendingFAQ scams](https://vendingfaq.com/scams.php); [VMFS USA](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/vending-machine-routes-for-sale-what-you-need-to-know-before-buying); [DealStream vending due diligence](https://dealstream.com/industry-guides/vending-routes/due-diligence)
- SOURCED: SOP 50 10 8.1 (Oct 2026) requires acquisition loans to be underwritten on historical, not projected, cash flow. — [MMCG Invest](https://www.mmcginvest.com/post/sba-stopped-lending-on-projections-sop-50-10-8-1-closes-acquisition-loans-on-last-year-s-numbers)

### Inferences (ESTIMATE, due-diligence checklist for a $10K buyer)
1. Match 3 years of federal returns (Schedule C or 1120S) to bank deposits. Reject any SDE that exists only in the broker's memo (CIM).
2. For routes: ride along on the route. Get written location or service agreements, check whether they are assignable, and check their termination clauses. Call the top 5 accounts, with seller consent, under a non-disclosure agreement. Get telemetry or meter reads for vending and ATMs.
3. Churn test: compare the current account list with the list from 12 and 24 months ago.
4. Customer concentration: cap any single account at under 20% or reprice.
5. Structure: put part of the price in a seller note that the buyer can offset against if revenue misrepresentation is found. Tie a transition or consulting period and a non-compete to the note.
6. Treat listings priced far below 1x SDE, like the $12,977 "automated cleaning" listing claiming $50–100K cash flow, as presumptively lead-gen or marketing packages, not operating businesses.
- Disproof: if Austin listings under $100K routinely come with 3 years of clean tax returns and assignable contracts, the risk discount above is overstated.

### Gaps
- No Texas-specific data on business-sale fraud or complaints (Texas AG or FTC) was found.
- No data on churn rates for vending or cleaning-contract routes was found.

## B1. Which B2B service niches in Austin show demand and thin local supply?

### Takeaway
Demand evidence is strongest for (1) commercial refrigeration and kitchen equipment repair, (2) EV charger maintenance and repair, (3) bookkeeping for contractors, and (4) office and apartment micro-markets. None has proven thin supply in Austin. Kitchen repair, micro-markets and shredding all have visible local incumbents. The best fits for $10K are skills-based services that need few assets: contractor bookkeeping, and refrigeration or kitchen repair if the operator is already trained. Asset-heavy niches (shredding, micro-markets at scale, laundromats) do not fit $10K.

### Cited Findings by niche

**1. Office and apartment vending / micro-markets**
- SOURCED (demand): U.S. convenience services revenue was about $31.1B in 2025, up from $26.6B in 2023 (8.1% annual growth). Micro-markets grew from $2.4B (2019) to about $5.4B (2023), rising from 9% to 20% of the industry, and are projected at 19% annual growth for 2023–2028. — [NAMA census press release](https://namanow.org/new-census-reveals-shifts-in-convenience-services-industry/); [GlobeNewswire, Mar 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/20/3259860/0/en/report-convenience-services-outpacing-much-of-foodservice-and-shifting-toward-tech-enabled-health-forward-offerings.html); [NAMA Census PDF](https://namanow.org/wp-content/uploads/NAMA-Census-FINAL.pdf)
- SOURCED (startup cost): A basic micro-market for about 200 people costs about $10–12K in equipment. A countertop kiosk with bill acceptor is about $7,500. A single-door cooler is $2,000–3,200. A full 500-person installation is $25–40K. — [VMFS USA micro markets vs vending 2026](https://vmfsusa.com/blogs/business/micro-markets-vs-vending-machines); [365 Retail Markets](https://365retailmarkets.com/blog/3-things-consider-converting-vending-markets); [The Vending Club](https://www.thevendingclub.net/pages/micro-markets-guide)
- SOURCED (Austin competition): Multiple operators actively market "zero-cost" micro-markets and vending to Austin offices and apartments. These include Austin Micro Market Vending, Nano Market ATX (apartments, offices, gyms), Greater Vending (Austin and Cedar Park), ReadySnacks (San Antonio and Austin), GrabGo ATX, All Star Austin Vending and a vendingexchange Austin affiliate. — [austinmicrovending.com](https://austinmicrovending.com/); [Nano Market ATX](https://www.nanomarketatx.com/blog); [Greater Vending Austin](https://greatervending.com/locations/austin/micro-markets/); [ReadySnacks](https://readysnackssa.com/micro-markets/); [GrabGo ATX](https://grabgoatx.com/); [All Star Austin Vending](https://allstaraustinvending.com/)
- ESTIMATE: Supply is not thin. $10K funds one small micro-market or 2–4 used machines. The revenue model is product markup at a venue with a commission or free placement. Disproof: if Austin property managers report no unserved Class B/C apartments or light-industrial sites, or operators' "zero-cost" offers are winning every placement, a new entrant has no edge.

**2. Commercial refrigeration / restaurant kitchen equipment repair**
- SOURCED (labor supply): The trade reports a shortage of new technicians, with a large share of the workforce near retirement. CFESA runs 5-day entry courses and certification in electricity, gas, steam and refrigeration to address it. — [RSI DFW](https://rsidfw.com/commercial-refrigeration-technician-career/); [CFESA](https://cfesa.com/); [CFESA courses](https://cfesa.com/courses/). This is qualitative trade-association and employer messaging, and no quantified 2025 shortage figure was found.
- SOURCED (Austin competition and response times): Austin providers include 512 Refrigeration (which cites a customer who got 24–48h quotes from competitors), America's Appliance Repair (same-day "when possible"), Baro Service, Commercial Kitchen (serving since the 1980s) and Restaurant Repair Company (24/7). — [512 Refrigeration](https://512refrigeration.com/commercial-kitchen-equipment-repair/); [America's Appliance Austin](https://americasapplianceaustin.com/industries/restaurants/); [Baro Service](https://www.baroserviceaustin.com/austin-tx/commercial-restaurant-equipment/); [Commercial Kitchen](https://commercialkitchen.com/locations/austin-tx/); [Restaurant Repair Co.](https://restaurant-repair-san-antonio.com/rrc-austin/)
- ESTIMATE: This is a workable $10K start only for someone already trained. The kit would be tools, gauges and recovery equipment plus a used van they already own. The revenue model is hourly or trip charges plus preventive-maintenance contracts with restaurants. Refrigeration work in Texas likely requires EPA 608 certification and a TDLR air-conditioning and refrigeration contractor license (UNVERIFIED, see Gaps). The advertised "24–48h" competitor response times suggest that fast response is the gap, not the lack of any provider. Disproof: Austin restaurants get same-day service reliably, or the licensing and insurance timeline exceeds budget.

**3. EV charger maintenance and repair**
- SOURCED (demand): J.D. Power 2025 found 14% of EV owners visited a charger without successfully charging, down from 19% in 2024. ChargerHelp's 2025 report found nearly one-third of charging attempts fail across 100,000+ sessions and 2,400 chargers. 35% of failures occurred on chargers that "appeared operational". Issues needing multiple work orders took a median of 32 days to resolve. — [Tech Brew on ChargerHelp, Oct 2025](https://www.techbrew.com/stories/2025/10/03/chargerhelp-report-ev-charging); [EVChargingStations on J.D. Power](https://evchargingstations.com/chargingnews/j-d-power-ev-chargers-becoming-more-reliable/); [Autoblog](https://www.autoblog.com/news/broken-chargers-are-becoming-americas-next-big-ev-challenge)
- SOURCED (Austin): The Austin Energy Plug-In EVerywhere network advertises 1,400+ Level 2 ports. Smart Charge America has held Austin Energy's service and maintenance contract. A related city solicitation, RFP 1100 DCM3038, reportedly closed Sept 18, 2025. — [Austin Energy EVerything Charging](https://austinenergy.com/green-power/plug-in-austin/EVerything-Charging); [Smart Charge America Austin](https://smartchargeamerica.com/locations/austin/); [City of Austin posting](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=468409) (via search summary; the RFP detail is unconfirmed)
- ESTIMATE: Demand is real, but the utility contract is already held by an incumbent. A small operator's opening is private property-owner chargers at apartments, offices and hotels, where the owner has no maintenance contract. Charger repair almost certainly requires a Texas electrical license (UNVERIFIED). A $10K start is feasible only for a licensed electrician or as a subcontractor to national field-service networks. Disproof: if charger OEMs and networks bundle maintenance through their own national field techs and Austin property owners already have warranties.

**4. Bookkeeping for trades contractors**
- SOURCED (supply shortage): U.S. accounting bachelor's and master's degrees fell to 55,152 in 2023–24, down 6.6% and a 20-year low. BLS projects about 115,300 accounting and auditing openings per year. AICPA/NASBA describe a "pipeline crisis", and about 25% of CPA firms outsource some bookkeeping. — [Ramp](https://ramp.com/blog/accountant-shortage); [Acobloom](https://www.acobloom.com/us/blog/understanding-the-us-accountant-shortage/). These are aggregators; the AICPA Trends report itself was not read.
- ESTIMATE: This fits $10K well. Costs are software, insurance, a construction-specific accounting certification or training, and marketing, at about $2–5K. The revenue model is monthly retainers; contractor work such as job costing, work-in-progress (WIP) schedules and pay-application billing supports higher fees than generic bookkeeping. Austin specifics: the region's large construction base is plausible but was not quantified in this research. Disproof: if Austin contractor bookkeeping is saturated by remote and offshore providers competing at under $300/month, or if contractors surveyed report no difficulty finding bookkeepers.

**5. Commercial document shredding**
- SOURCED (Austin competition): Shred-it, RW Lone Star, A-Tex Shredding, Central Texas Shredding (local) and Annex/records-storage brokers all serve Austin. **6. Dental / medical OSHA and HIPAA compliance training**

**7. Small-office IT / managed services, and 8. drive-thru / dumpster-pad pressure washing**

### Inferences (ESTIMATE, ranking for a $10K Austin operator)
1. **Contractor bookkeeping.** Best capital fit, with a sourced structural talent shortage. Local competition is unmeasured.
2. **Commercial refrigeration / kitchen repair PM contracts.** Sourced labor-shortage narrative, and Austin incumbents advertise 24–48h competitor delays. Requires existing skill and licensing.
3. **EV charger maintenance for private property owners.** Strong sourced reliability gap. The Austin utility contract is taken, and the work requires an electrical license.
4. **Micro-markets / vending.** Strongest sourced industry growth, but visibly crowded in Austin, and $10K buys only one site.
5. **Shredding and compliance training.** Crowded or commoditized. Only viable through acquisition or as a broker.
