# Government & Public-Sector Procurement as a Route to a <$10K Austin-Metro Business (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop) — research notes, compiled 2026-09-28

Method note: most primary government domains (austintexas.gov, services.austintexas.gov, comptroller.texas.gov, capmetro.org, api.usaspending.gov) and several news/law-firm sites were blocked by the egress proxy in this environment. Findings below come from search-result text of those pages (primary documents quoted via search snippets) plus secondary sources. I did not circumvent the blocks. As a result **no USAspending place-of-performance pull was possible** and **no AFO solicitation-level bid-count dataset was assembled**. Everything labeled SOURCED has a link; everything labeled ESTIMATE/INFERENCE is my reasoning.

**Biggest non-obvious finding:** 2025–2026 changed the rules underneath this whole strategy. (1) Texas SB 1173 doubled the local formal-bid threshold from $50K to $100K (effective Sept 1, 2025). (2) The Texas HUB program was turned into "VetHUB" (service-disabled veterans only). About 15,000 minority- and women-owned HUBs were decertified, then a Travis County court partly restored them by injunction (April 2026). The state appealed and trial is set for Nov 9, 2026. (3) USDOT removed race and sex presumptions from DBE certification (Oct 3, 2025), which freezes DBE goal-setting at ABIA and CapMetro until firms are re-evaluated. (4) Army Futures Command became T2COM (HQ Austin, Oct 2, 2025). (5) The federal micro-purchase threshold rose to $15K and the SAT rose to $350K (Oct 1, 2025). A newcomer who understands these shifts has an information edge over incumbents.

## Q1. Purchasing thresholds (micro / informal / formal) for City of Austin, Travis County & surrounding counties, school districts, CapMetro, Texas state agencies, federal buyers — and what's bought below formal bid

### Takeaway
Since Sept 1, 2025, Texas law lets cities, counties, school districts and transit authorities buy up to **$100,000** without formal sealed bids (it was $50,000). State agencies still post anything over **$25,000** on the ESBD. Federal buyers can use a card for up to **$15,000** and simplified procedures up to **$350,000**. For a micro-business, the realistic target is the informal-quote band. For local governments that band now runs from about $3K to $100K, and state law requires cities in that band to contact at least two HUBs.

### Cited Findings
**Texas local governments (cities, counties, ISDs, transit authorities): statutory**
- SB 1173 (89th Leg.) raised the municipal competitive-bidding threshold in Local Gov't Code §252.021(a) from $50,000 to **$100,000**, **effective Sept 1, 2025**. The previous increase was in 2007, from $25K to $50K. — [TML Legislative Update, June 20, 2025](https://www.tml.org/1053/June-20-2025-Number-24); [Senate Research Center bill analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/pdf/SB01173F.pdf)
- SB 1173 also amends LGC §262.023(a) for counties, raising it from $50K to $100K. One summary says the bill applies to "school districts, municipalities, counties, and transit authorities." — [Randle Law Office summary](https://www.randlelawoffice.com/local-governmental-entities/texas-sb-1173-doubles-competitive-bidding-threshold/); [Texas Association of Counties – County Purchasing Act changes](https://www.county.org/resources/news/legalease-faqs-by-subject/county-finances/changes-county-purchasing-act)
- Under LGC §252.0215 as amended, a city spending **more than $3,000 but less than $100,000** must "contact at least two historically underutilized businesses on a rotating basis." — [TML, June 20, 2025](https://www.tml.org/1053/June-20-2025-Number-24); [SRC analysis](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/89R/analysis/pdf/SB01173F.pdf)

**City of Austin (as published; may predate SB 1173)**
- $5,000.01 up to the competitive threshold uses an **informal solicitation / Invitation for Quote (IFQ)**, paid via a Central Purchase Order or Master Agreement and approved by Central Procurement. Above the threshold, the City uses a formal advertised solicitation approved by **City Council through a Recommendation for Council Action (RCA)**. City material states the range as **$5,000–$50,000** (document date not visible; likely pre-Sept 2025). — [City of Austin Procurement Process presentation](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=443570); [Asian Contractors Assn. summary of City process](https://www.acta-austin.com/doc/Procurement.pdf); [City IFQ example, July 2024](https://media.governmentnavigator.com/media/bid/1720645550_2024-07-10_4400_PC1011.pdf)
- A citizen-portal summary of a City meeting describes "competitive sealed bidding, **$50,000 IFB threshold**." The page could not be fetched and its date is unknown. — [Citizen Portal](https://citizenportal.ai/articles/6699126/texas/travis-county/austin/city-procurement-staff-explain-competitive-sealed-bidding-50000-ifb-threshold)
- SMBR material says "All solicitations over **$58,000** must be reviewed by SMBR" (older SMBR doc; date unknown). — [SMBR MBE/WBE Goal Setting presentation](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=267525)
- **CONFLICT:** City sources say $50K, while the statute now allows $100K. I found no source confirming whether Austin has raised its internal formal threshold to $100K. A City can keep a lower internal threshold by policy.

**Travis County**
- The County's purchasing rules are in Travis County Code Ch. 32. I could not retrieve specific dollar bands. The statutory county formal threshold moved from $50K to $100K on Sept 1, 2025. — [Travis County Code Ch. 32](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/traviscounty-tx/doc-viewer.aspx?secid=880); [Travis County Purchasing Office](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/purchasing)

**Austin ISD**
- AISD gets quotes from several vendors for goods between **$3,500 and $50,000**. For **$5,000.01–$10,000** it solicits at least three informal bids, and **two of the three must be sought from HUBs**. Figures are as published on the AISD site, probably not yet updated for SB 1173. — [AISD Solicitation Process](https://www.austinisd.org/cp/doing-business/process)
- Registering with AISD only triggers solicitation notices. It "does not automatically make you an approved vendor." A Conflict of Interest Questionnaire (CIQ) must be filed within 7 days of starting negotiations or submitting a bid. — [AISD Vendor FAQ](https://www.austinisd.org/cp/cis/vendor-faq)

**Capital Metro**
- CapMetro has two small-purchase tiers: **under $10,000**, and **$10,000–$100,000**. Anything over **$100,000** requires full and open competition. CapMetro runs DBE and SBE programs through its Small Business Programs & Compliance dept (contact procure-help@capmetro.org, 512-389-7460). — [CapMetro Vendor Guide](https://www.capmetro.org/vendors); [CapMetro Purchasing](https://www.capmetro.org/purchasing); [CapMetro DBE](https://www.capmetro.org/dbe)

**Texas state agencies (many HQ'd in Austin)**
- **Spot purchase**: a delegated, non-competitive method for goods or services **not exceeding $10,000**. — [Texas OAG Procurement Methods](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/procurement/procurement-methods); [OAG Procurement Handbook](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/sites/default/files/files/divisions/procurement/OAG-PCO-Handbook.pdf)
- **$5,000.01–$25,000 informal bids**: the agency must contact **two certified HUB vendors and one non-HUB vendor** (per the Comptroller's Procurement & Contract Management Guide v1.2 as cited by agencies). **Over $25,000**: posted to the **Electronic State Business Daily (ESBD)**, with CMBL vendors in the matching NIGP class notified. — [HHSC HUB Procurement Thresholds](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/doing-business-with-hhs/contracting/hub/hub-procurement-thresholds.pdf); [GLO Procurement Policy](https://www.glo.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2025-09/Procurement-Policy_Rev.-August%202024.pdf)
- **CMBL** registration costs **$70/year**. Agencies must search the CMBL when planning buys. — [GovUnpacked TX vendor guide](https://www.govunpacked.com/blog/texas-state-vendor-registration); [Outrider TX guide](https://outrider.app/state-guides/government-contracting-in-texas); [Comptroller CMBL page](https://www.comptroller.texas.gov/purchasing/vendor/cmbl)

**Federal**
- Effective **Oct 1, 2025**, the micro-purchase threshold rose from **$10,000 to $15,000** and the simplified acquisition threshold (SAT) from **$250,000 to $350,000**. The Davis-Bacon (construction) micro-purchase stays at **$2,000**. The prime subcontracting-plan threshold rose from $750K to **$900K**. The commercial simplified-procedures ceiling rose from $7.5M to $9M. — [GSA SmartPay Smart Bulletin 002](https://smartpay.gsa.gov/guidance-and-audits/smart-bulletins/002/); [Federal Register, Aug 27, 2025](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/08/27/2025-16412/federal-acquisition-regulation-inflation-adjustment-of-acquisition-related-thresholds); [SmallGovCon](https://smallgovcon.com/statutes-and-regulations/finalized-increases-to-micro-purchase-simplified-acquisition-and-other-thresholds/)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: SB 1173 widened the informal band by roughly $50K at every local buyer in the four counties, including Williamson, Hays and Bastrop counties, Round Rock, San Marcos and the ISDs. More work is now bought by informal quotes that are often emailed only to a department's known-vendor list. **Being on that list, through relationships with department buyers, matters more than monitoring public bid boards.**
- INFERENCE: In the local $3K–$100K band and the state $5K–$25K band, the statutes require buyers to contact HUBs. After the Jan 2026 decertifications (Q2), buyers' HUB lists shrank sharply. Any vendor that holds current HUB/VetHUB status is therefore more likely to be called for informal quotes.
- ESTIMATE: Services most often bought informally by these buyers are one-off or small recurring jobs, for example: office moves and furniture reconfiguration, event and meeting AV setup, translation of notices, pest control at single facilities, small signage orders, document scanning or shredding purges, specialty cleaning (post-construction, carpet, window), courier runs, and small repairs. I could not verify frequencies from AFO or ESBD data (blocked).

### Gaps
- City of Austin and Travis County internal thresholds after SB 1173 (did either raise to $100K?). The City's Purchasing Manual and Travis County Code Ch. 32 text could not be fetched.
- Williamson, Hays and Bastrop county, UT Austin and Round Rock ISD specific thresholds were not retrieved. UT Austin, as a state institution of higher education, has its own delegated procurement rules and likely different bands; unverified.
- No data on count or dollar value of sub-threshold purchases by category (AFO / Texas Transparency / USAspending blocked).

## Q2. Texas HUB certification (eligibility, cost, time, goals vs actuals) and City of Austin MBE/WBE / DBE programs — do primes struggle to find certified subs?

### Takeaway
The HUB landscape is in legal flux as of Sept 2026. In Dec 2025 the Comptroller turned HUB into **VetHUB**, limited to businesses owned by veterans with a 20%+ service-connected disability. About **15,000 of 15,790 HUBs were decertified** on Jan 6, 2026. A **Travis County district court temporarily enjoined** the change in April 2026. The state appealed, adopted permanent VetHUB rules in May 2026, and final trial is set for **Nov 9, 2026**. The City of Austin runs its own MBE/WBE program (City Code Ch. 2-9) with goals of roughly 14–16%. Federal DBE (used at ABIA and CapMetro) is frozen pending mass re-certification under USDOT's Oct 2025 interim rule. For a founder who is a **20%+ service-disabled veteran**, this is an unusual scarcity window. For others, certification-based strategies are currently unreliable.

### Cited Findings
**Texas HUB → VetHUB timeline**
- Historically, a HUB was a business at least **51% owned and controlled by economically disadvantaged person(s)**. — [NAACP LDF explainer](https://www.naacpldf.org/case-issue/texas-statewide-historically-underutilized-business-hub-program-explained/)
- **Oct 2025**: the Comptroller froze the HUB program. **Dec 2, 2025**: Acting Comptroller Kelly Hancock announced emergency rules renaming it **Veteran Heroes United in Business (VetHUB)**, limited to small businesses owned by veterans with a **≥20% service-connected disability**. — [Comptroller press release, Dec 2, 2025](https://comptroller.texas.gov/about/media-center/news/20251202-acting-texas-comptroller-kelly-hancock-announces-emergency-rules-for-revamped-vethub-program-1764695317378); [everythinglubbock/Nexstar](https://www.everythinglubbock.com/news/latest/thousands-lost-hub-status-now-texas-must-decide-what-comes-next/)
- **Jan 6, 2026**: the Comptroller canceled all HUBs owned by minorities and women, notifying only some by email. — [Jackson Lewis](https://www.jacksonlewis.com/insights/minority-and-women-owned-businesses-sue-texas-comptroller-over-contracting-programs-conversion-cancellation). (Note: the same source's snippet dates the emergency regulation "January 2, 2025," which conflicts with the Comptroller's Dec 2, 2025 release. It is likely a typo.)
- **Scale**: in the first six months of FY2025 there were **15,790** HUB-certified businesses, of which **485** were owned by male service-disabled veterans. More than **15,000 (~97%)** owned by people of color and women lost certification. Comptroller data in **April 2026** showed **1,513 VetHUB-certified businesses**. — [everythinglubbock/Nexstar](https://www.everythinglubbock.com/news/latest/thousands-lost-hub-status-now-texas-must-decide-what-comes-next/); [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/judge-temporarily-blocks-changes-to-the-historically-underutilized-business-program/)
- **FY2025 actuals**: HUBs won "roughly **$2 billion**" in awards, about **11%** of total state procurement spending. — [NAACP LDF](https://www.naacpldf.org/case-issue/texas-statewide-historically-underutilized-business-hub-program-explained/) (via search summary)
- **Statewide goals** (good-faith-effort targets by category), as reported: **11.2%** construction, **23.7%** other services, **26%** professional services, **21.1%** commodities. — [NAACP LDF](https://www.naacpldf.org/case-issue/texas-statewide-historically-underutilized-business-hub-program-explained/). **CAUTION:** this is a search-summary rendering. My unverified recollection of 34 TAC §20.284 is heavy construction 11.2%, building construction 21.1%, special trade 32.9%, professional services 23.7%, other services 26.0%, commodities 21.1%, which suggests the snippet swapped the "other services" and "professional services" figures. Verify against the rule text.
- **Litigation**: suit filed **Mar 2, 2026** by four businesses plus the Houston chapter of the National Association of Minority Contractors (two more plaintiffs joined **Mar 13, 2026**). A **Travis County District Court granted a temporary injunction in April 2026** (reported Apr 14), finding the Comptroller "lacks the authority to determine the constitutionality of the HUB Act" and that the rules would cause "immediate, irreparable injury." — [Texas Tribune, Mar 2, 2026](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/03/02/texas-minority-women-business-hub-program-lawsuit-comptroller/); [Houston Public Media, Apr 14, 2026](https://www.houstonpublicmedia.org/articles/news/business/2026/04/14/549035/judge-temporarily-reinstates-women-minority-owned-businesses-into-texas-hub-program/); [Austin Chronicle](https://www.austinchronicle.com/news/historically-underutilized-business-program-temporarily-restored/); [GBEF](https://gbef.com/post/gbef-wins-temporary-injunction)
- The state filed a **notice of appeal**. Permanent VetHUB rules replaced the emergency rules around **May 5–12, 2026**; sources differ on the exact date. **Final trial is set for Nov 9, 2026.** — [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/news/texas-politics/judge-temporarily-blocks-changes-to-the-historically-underutilized-business-program/); [Off the Kuff](https://www.offthekuff.com/wp/?p=122987); [GBEF "The HUB Case"](https://gbef.com/the-hub-case)
- A Feb 2026 report says the Comptroller **cut the City of Austin out of state HUB certification**. The City had processed HUB certifications locally; the page could not be fetched for details. — [Hoodline, Feb 2026](https://hoodline.com/2026/02/state-snatches-hub-power-from-austin-city-hall/)

**City of Austin MBE/WBE (SMBR)**
- Program goals vary by category: **MBE 13.7% (construction) to 15.8% (professional services)**, **WBE 13.8% (construction) to 15.8% (professional services)** (SMBR presentation; date not visible). — [SMBR MBE/WBE Goal Setting](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=267525)
- All primes must either meet SMBR goals through subcontractors or be compliant via **Good Faith Efforts (GFE)**. GFE includes documenting the MBEs and WBEs contacted, plans and specs provided, local-publication notice, and reasons agreements failed. — [City of Austin MBE/WBE Procurement Packet](https://imlive.s3.amazonaws.com/Austin/ID297057938421313333710019968576558068966/CSP_6100_CLMB338_MWDBE_PAC_v1.pdf); [GFE Compliance Determination Form](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=260091)
- Example project-level result: a solicitation issued **Feb 26, 2026** and closed **Mar 30, 2026** received **15 offers**. It will be awarded under City Code Ch. 2-9B by meeting goals with **7.26% MBE and 3.15% WBE** participation. — [City of Austin RCA 26-1998](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=477100)
- The City publishes an open-data measure, "EOA.A.5 – **Prime Contractors Meeting SMBR Solicitation Goals**." It could not be fetched. — [City of Austin Open Data](https://data.austintexas.gov/stories/s/EOA-A-5-Prime-Contractors-Meeting-SMBR-Solicitatio/9q8x-qhyx/)
- An Oct 14, 2025 MBE/WBE annual update to the Mueller (RMMA) commission exists; the numbers could not be retrieved. — [RMMA PIAC Oct 14, 2025](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=461322)

**Federal DBE (affects ABIA airport and CapMetro, both federally funded)**
- USDOT's **Interim Final Rule of Oct 3, 2025** removed race- and sex-based presumptions of disadvantage. All DBE/ACDBE firms must re-certify with individualized narratives and new personal-net-worth documentation. **Agencies, including the City of Austin, may not set DBE/ACDBE contract goals or count participation until re-evaluations are complete.** — [City of Austin notice](https://www.austintexas.gov/article/important-updates-dbe-and-acdbe-certification-requirements); [CRS IN12627](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN12627); [Miller Nash](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/usdot-issues-new-dbe-renewal-rules-no-more-automatic-race-or-gender-presumptions-to-qualify)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: A **service-disabled veteran (≥20%)** founder could hold one of only ~1,513 VetHUB certifications statewide (Apr 2026), against ~15,790 HUBs a year earlier. State agencies must still make good-faith efforts toward HUB goals. Primes on state contracts over $100K (HUB Subcontracting Plan threshold, from my knowledge and not re-verified here) also need HUB subs or GFE documentation. That founder therefore becomes one of the few certified subs primes and agency buyers can call. **This is the single strongest certification-based opening found.** Disproved if the Nov 9, 2026 trial or an appellate ruling fully restores the ~15,000 decertified HUBs, or if agencies stop tracking HUB goals.
- INFERENCE: For non-veterans, relying on HUB, DBE or 8(a) status in year one is risky. Status may be restored, re-litigated or re-certified on a new basis within the year. The City of Austin MBE/WBE program under City Code Ch. 2-9 is a separate, city-run program and was still being applied in 2026 RCAs (above), so it remains the most stable minority- and women-owned lever locally.
- INFERENCE (weak): The 7.26% MBE / 3.15% WBE achieved on a 2026 contract is below the 13.7–15.8% program-level goals. This suggests project goals are set lower, or that primes lean on GFE, which is consistent with scarce certified subs in some trades. I could not identify which trades from accessible sources.

### Gaps
- VetHUB application **cost and processing time**. My prior knowledge is that HUB certification was free, with roughly 30–90 days of processing; this is unverified for VetHUB (comptroller.texas.gov blocked).
- Whether the April 2026 temporary injunction is currently in effect or superseded by the state's appeal. Under Texas practice a governmental appeal can supersede an injunction; unconfirmed.
- Which trades or services Austin primes cite most in GFE waivers. The EOA.A.5 dashboard and SMBR availability data were not retrievable.
- City of Austin MBE/WBE certification cost and time. There is also a 2025–26 status question (whether Austin moved certification away from state-HUB reciprocity after the Feb 2026 change).

## Q3. Federal: SAM.gov cost/time, set-asides, and federal spending in the Austin area

### Takeaway
SAM.gov registration is **free**. It takes about **10 business days at best**, and **several weeks to 10+ weeks** if entity validation snags. The biggest local federal buyer shift is **Army Futures Command's merger into T2COM**, HQ'd in Austin since **Oct 2, 2025** with space in Round Rock. Federal set-aside programs are being restructured (8(a) audit, 1,000+ suspensions, race-neutral rule). For a micro-business, the realistic federal entry is the **$15K micro-purchase / government purchase card** tier, not formal set-asides.

### Cited Findings
- SAM.gov registration and UEI are **free**. Activation can take **up to 10 business days** after submission, but entity validation (IRS name mismatches, address standardization) adds **5–15** and **3–10 business days** respectively. One source says 2025–26 changes pushed typical validation from **6–8 weeks to 10+ weeks** for many applicants. These are secondary, vendor-published sources of varying quality; the 10-week claim comes from a registration-services firm. — [USFCR blog (2026)](https://blogs.usfcr.com/sam-registration-2025); [Federal Processing Registry](https://federalprocessingregistry.net/sam-registration-timeline/); [SLED.AI guide](https://www.sledai.com/blog/sam-registration-guide/)
- **Army Futures Command** (HQ Austin, founded Aug 24, 2018) was **disbanded Oct 2, 2025** and merged with TRADOC into **Army Transformation and Training Command (T2COM)**, headquartered in Austin, with ~350,000 personnel command-wide. AFC also established space in **Round Rock**. Leadership was in flux in April 2026, when Gen. Hodne was removed. — [Wikipedia: T2COM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Transformation_and_Training_Command); [AUSA](https://www.ausa.org/news/army-stands-transformation-and-training-command); [Defense News, Oct 13, 2025](https://www.defensenews.com/land/2025/10/13/inside-the-us-armys-new-modernization-mega-command/); [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/army-t2com-afc-tradoc-merger); [The Defense Post, May 29, 2026](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/05/29/t2com-integration-challenge/)
- The Army Applications Laboratory sits in Capital Factory downtown. GAO (2019) urged AFC to improve small-business engagement for R&D. — [GAO-19-511](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-19-511); [Defense News 2019](https://www.defensenews.com/land/2019/07/22/can-futures-command-change-the-armys-relationship-with-academia-and-small-business/)
- **8(a) program**: SBA began its first-ever program-wide audit in **June 2025**. It **suspended 1,000+ participants on Jan 22, 2026** for failing to produce records, and on **Jun 11, 2026** announced reforms requiring individualized proof of social disadvantage (no race-based presumption). DoW (DoD) announced line-by-line review of small-business awards over $20M. — [SBA, Jun 11, 2026](https://www.sba.gov/article/2026/06/11/sba-reforms-8a-business-development-program-end-racial-discrimination-federal-contracting); [Bass Berry](https://www.bassberrygovcontrade.com/sba-8a-audit-data-dow-small-business-awards-review/); [Forbes, Jan 27, 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/nataliemadeiracofield/2026/01/27/1000-firms-out-65-in-whats-happening-to-the-8a-program/)
- Federal thresholds effective Oct 1, 2025: micro-purchase **$15K**, SAT **$350K** (see Q1). — [GSA SmartPay](https://smartpay.gsa.gov/guidance-and-audits/smart-bulletins/002/)

### Inferences
- INFERENCE: A new downtown or Round Rock command HQ in integration mode generates card-level needs: office moves and reconfiguration, furniture assembly, conference and event AV, courier runs, small signage, and shredding purges during consolidation. Each fits under $15K, so it can be bought with a government purchase card without competition. The federal micro-purchase threshold does not require a set-aside. Disproved if T2COM's Austin footprint is served mainly through existing GSA facilities contracts or base-support IDIQs, or through the lessor under a full-service lease.
- INFERENCE: Formal federal set-asides (8(a), WOSB, HUBZone) are a poor year-one bet for a <$10K firm in 2026. They are in regulatory churn, and the APEX-cited 12–18-month average to a first award (Q5) exceeds the runway.

### Gaps
- **Federal award data by place of performance (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop), by NAICS and award size, was not retrievable.** The USAspending API and website were blocked. The report writer should treat "which federal service categories show repeated small awards in Austin" as **unanswered**.
- VA Austin (outpatient clinic and records center), GSA facilities in Austin, and the IRS Austin campus: current footprint and spending not found.
- Camp Mabry is a Texas Military Department (state) facility. Its purchasing was not researched.

## Q4. Specific service categories repeatedly bought that a solo operator could supply for <$10K startup — evidence of few bidders / re-solicitation

### Takeaway
Direct evidence of thin competition exists in City of Austin RCAs: several 2025–26 solicitations drew **one or two** responsive offers. Most of those are capital- or license-heavy (ABIA grease-trap pumping, security systems), so they are not <$10K opportunities. Solicitation-level data for the small informal buys was not retrievable. The candidates below are therefore **ESTIMATES** ranked by fit, each with a disproof test.

### Cited Findings
- **ABIA grease-trap / grit-trap service**: solicitation issued **Jan 6, 2025**, closed **Feb 20, 2025**, drew **only two offers**. Awarded to Southwaste Disposal LLC, 5 years, **NTE $1,100,000**. — [City of Austin RCA 25-0742](https://services.austintexas.gov/edims/document.cfm?id=451158). A contractor worker **died after falling into an ABIA grease trap**, and the family has a legal claim against the City. — [KXAN](https://www.kxan.com/investigations/city-notified-of-legal-claim-after-worker-died-falling-into-austin-airport-grease-trap/); [KVUE](https://www.kvue.com/article/news/local/269-5069145c-1e62-4adc-a99f-06ba9649922e)
- Other City of Austin RCAs showing **one responsive offer**: RFP **8100 SAR3012** (published May 26, 2025, closed Jun 24, 2025) and IFB **6200 TJP1025** (Dec 8, 2025 to Jan 20, 2026). The service categories were not visible in snippets. RCA **25-2042** extended a deadline and still got only one offer (Convergint Technologies, a security/systems integrator). ## Q5. What it realistically takes (time to first award, payment terms, insurance/bonding) and common failure modes

### Takeaway
Plan on **12–18 months** to a first government award. Texas public buyers are required to pay within **30 days**, with automatic interest thereafter. Insurance requirements (typically GL $500K–$1M, commercial auto ~$1M CSL, and workers' comp or an alternative for on-site work) are the main recurring cost. The main failure modes are running out of runway before the first award, wrong or unmaintained registrations, and chasing formal bids instead of informal-quote relationships.

### Cited Findings
- On average it takes **12–18 months** to secure a first government contract, though some firms win sooner. APEX Accelerators provide **no-cost** counseling, including in Texas. The UTSA APEX Accelerator serves much of Central/South Texas; the Austin-serving center was not confirmed. — [GT APEX Accelerator FAQ (Georgia; general guidance)](https://gtapexaccelerator.org/frequently-asked-questions/); [UTSA APEX Accelerator](https://apexaccelerator.iedtexas.org/); [APEX Accelerators locator](https://www.apexaccelerators.us/)
- **Texas Prompt Payment Act (Gov't Code Ch. 2251)**: payment is due **30 days** after the later of receipt of goods/services or a correct invoice. Payments are overdue on the 31st day and accrue interest at **1% plus prime**, calculated automatically. This applies to state agencies including universities (e.g., UT Austin). — [Texas Comptroller eXpendit – Prompt Payment](https://fmx.cpa.texas.gov/fm/pubs/purchase/prompt_pay/index.php); [UTSA policy 0704](https://www.utsa.edu/financialaffairs/resources/financial-guidelines/0704.html); [Texas State Univ.](https://www.txst.edu/gao/ap/resources/prompt-pay.html)
- **Insurance** (weak, secondary sources; insurer marketing): City of Austin permit/registration contexts commonly require GL of **$500K–$1M** (one source says a minimum of $300K for DSD registration). Commercial auto of **$1M CSL** is common, with some at **$2M**. Texas does not mandate workers' comp, but some City registrations require WC or an approved alternative. The Texas Council Risk Management Fund publishes model vendor insurance guidelines for Texas local governments. — [Construction Pros Insurance](https://www.constructionprosinc.com/austin); [Heaton Bennett Insurance](https://insureaustin.com/contractor-insurance-austin-tx-local-regulations-and-options/); [TCRMF insurance guidelines](https://www.tcrmf.org/resources-tcrmf-actual-content/loss-prevention-bulletins/insurance-guidelines-contractors-service-providers-vendors/). GL cost for Austin contractors is cited at **$150–$400/month** (2026, marketing source). — [contractorsinsured.net](https://contractorsinsured.net/blog/general-liability-insurance-cost-austin-texas/)
- AISD requires a **Conflict of Interest Questionnaire within 7 days** of engaging, and registration does not equal approved-vendor status. — [AISD Vendor FAQ](https://www.austinisd.org/cp/cis/vendor-faq)
- SAM.gov validation failures can stall federal eligibility for weeks to months (Q3). — [USFCR](https://blogs.usfcr.com/sam-registration-2025)
- Certification-program instability in 2025–26 (HUB → VetHUB, DBE re-certification freeze, 8(a) suspensions) is itself a live failure mode for firms whose pipeline depends on a certification (Q2, Q3).

### Inferences
- ESTIMATE, year-one cost stack for a service micro-business (within $10K):
  - LLC formation: ~$300 (Texas SOS filing $300, from prior knowledge).
  - CMBL: $70.
  - SAM: $0.
  - HUB/VetHUB: likely $0 (unverified).
  - GL: ~$1.8–4.8K/yr at the quoted $150–$400/mo (lower for non-construction services).
  - Hired/non-owned auto or commercial auto: ~$1–3K.
  - Bonding: generally not required below formal thresholds; performance/payment bonds attach to public-works contracts at higher values (Texas Gov't Code Ch. 2253, from prior knowledge; not verified here).
  - Equipment remainder: ~$2–5K.
  - Cash reserve: at least 45–60 days of payroll/supplies to bridge net-30 plus invoice-rejection delays.
- ESTIMATE, common failure modes:
  1. Running out of runway during the 12–18-month ramp.
  2. Bidding formal RFPs against incumbents instead of cultivating department buyers for informal quotes (now up to $100K locally).
  3. Invoices rejected for PO/format errors, which resets the 30-day clock.
  4. Under-insuring and losing awards at the certificate-of-insurance stage.
  5. Building the business around a certification that is being litigated or reworked.
  6. Safety and liability exposure in hazardous niches (see the ABIA grease-trap fatality).
- ESTIMATE, the realistic sequence: register everywhere in month 1 (AFO, CMBL, each county and ISD portal, SAM). Meet with an APEX counselor. Win micro/spot buys (≤$10K state spot, ≤$15K federal card, ≤~$5K City of Austin without quotes) in months 3–9 to build past-performance references. Only then pursue $25K–$100K informal quotes and sub slots.
