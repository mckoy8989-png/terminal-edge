## Cross-candidate summary and ranking

### Takeaway
Aerobic septic (OSSF) maintenance contracts are the best fit on the evidence. The law requires a visit every 4 months. Travis, Williamson and Hays enforce contracts and don't let homeowners maintain their own systems. Equipment costs are low. The catch is licensing: a newcomer probably can't hold contracts under their own license on day one. SWPPP construction inspections come second, because the state requires no license and every active site needs an inspection every 7–14 days. Registered Accessibility Specialist (RAS) work comes third, for anyone who meets TDLR's education rules. Backflow testing is confirmed as crowded, with $75 tests. Grease-trap pumping, elevator QEI inspection, fire sprinkler inspection and cooling-tower inspection don't fit a $10K newcomer start.

## Texas OSSF / aerobic septic maintenance (Travis, Williamson, Hays, Bastrop)

### Takeaway
The mandate is strong. Aerobic systems need a licensed maintenance contract with inspections every 4 months. Austin-area counties require continuous contracts and don't allow homeowners to self-maintain. Contracts sell for about $200–$500 a year (2026 figures). The key constraint for a newcomer is the TCEQ "licensed maintenance provider" pathway. It needs an Installer II license, a Class C or higher wastewater operator license, or 3 years as a registered maintenance provider or technician. The fastest legal entry may be registering as a maintenance technician and working under an existing licensed provider.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis (SOURCED):** Inspections must be performed by a TCEQ-licensed maintenance provider under a maintenance contract required by Health & Safety Code §366.0515. The provider must submit an inspection report to the county permitting authority after each visit. — [Texas Septic Guide, regulations (2026)](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/texas-septic-regulations); [TCEQ OSSF maintenance page](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/ossfmaintenance.html) (search snippet only)
- **Frequency (SOURCED):** Texas requires aerobic inspections every 4 months under a mandatory contract. — [Texas Septic Guide, maintenance contracts (2026)](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/septic-maintenance-contract-worth-it-texas). Texas requires 3 inspections per year by a licensed maintenance provider for the first two years on every aerobic system. — [SepticSeeker / TCEQ snippet](https://www.septicseeker.com/guides/septic-regulations-texas)
- **Travis County (SOURCED):** Travis County Code Chapter 448 requires a continuous licensed maintenance contract on aerobic systems, with reports filed every 4 months through the Travis County TNR online portal. — [Mac Septic](https://macseptic.com/aerobic-septic-permits-tceq-texas/); [Travis County Ch. 448](https://online.encodeplus.com/regs/traviscounty-tx/doc-viewer.aspx?secid=3575). Travis County affidavit text: after the initial two-year service policy, the owner of a single-family aerobic system must get a maintenance contract within 30 days or maintain it personally. — [Travis County SP-04 affidavit](https://www.traviscountytx.gov/images/tnr/Docs/SP-04.pdf). **Conflict:** another source says Williamson, Hays and Travis have stricter local rules that block homeowner maintenance entirely. — [Texas Septic Guide (2026)](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/septic-maintenance-contract-worth-it-texas). The affidavit wording and the Chapter 448 "continuous contract" wording disagree on Travis. Treat Travis's homeowner self-maintenance option as unresolved.
- **Hays County (SOURCED):** Ongoing maintenance is required for all aerobic or advanced-treatment OSSF systems. — [Hays County septic permits](https://www.hayscountytx.gov/septic-permits) (via search snippet)
- **Williamson County (SOURCED):** Requires all aerobic maintenance reports to be filed with Environmental Health. An estimated 25% of Williamson County households use OSSFs. — [Williamson County OSSF pages/brochure](https://www.wilcotx.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3057/OSSF-Care-Brochure-PDF); [WilCo Property Owner](https://www.wilcotx.gov/650/Property-Owner)
- **Travis County share (SOURCED, low quality):** An aggregator says an estimated 18% of Travis County homes use septic. — [SepticRoster / Septic & Well Pro listing pages](https://septicroster.com/texas/travis/). The search engine turned this into "~232,000 homes", but that is 18% of population, not homes. Treat it as unreliable.
- **Permit fees (SOURCED, 2026):** Williamson $510 conventional / $710 aerobic. Travis $285–$475 conventional / $475–$700 aerobic. — [Septic & Well Pro](https://septicandwell.com/articles/texas-septic-permit)
- **Statewide new OSSF permits (SOURCED):** 43,215 in 2024, 46,830 in 2023 and 54,593 in 2022. — [TCEQ historical OSSF permitting data](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/permitting/ossf/texas-historical-ossf-permitting-data) (search snippet)
- **Contract price (SOURCED, 2026 data):** $300–$500 a year typical. Other ranges quoted: $200–$500 and $300–$600. — [Texas Septic Guide cost pages](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/aerobic-septic-system-cost-texas). Hill Country and rural contracts run $300–$500 a year because of travel distances. Owners should budget $500–$800 a year including consumables. — [Texas Septic Guide aerobic maintenance (2026)](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/aerobic-septic-maintenance-texas). Another aggregator gives $150–$500 a year. — [Texas Septic Guide contract page](https://www.texassepticguide.com/learn/septic-maintenance-contract-worth-it-texas). An Austin provider publishes a maintenance-contracts page. — [VSE Septic Services](https://www.vsesepticservicesllc.com/maintenance-contracts)
- **License pathway (SOURCED):** A licensed maintenance provider must hold a current Installer Class II (OSII) license or a Class C or higher Wastewater Operator license, or have completed three years as a registered maintenance provider or maintenance technician. Every person doing maintenance under a licensed provider must be a registered maintenance technician who has completed the TCEQ-approved Basic Maintenance Provider course. — [TCEQ training/licensing snippets](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/licensing/training); [TCEQ OSSF licenses](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/licensing/licenses/ossflic)
- **Training (SOURCED):** The Basic Maintenance Provider (BMP) and Advanced Maintenance Provider (AMP) courses are both required for the license. The AMP must include at least 16 hours of instruction. — [TCEQ Advanced MP curriculum guidance](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/licensing/occupational/training-providers/advanced-ossf-mp-curriculum-guidance.pdf). The BMP course costs $340 per student at one provider. — [Sure Nuff Septic Training](https://surenuffseptictraining.com/basic-maintenance-provider-training-course/). Renewal needs 24 CE hours. — [TXOWA FAQ](https://txowa.org/faq/) (snippet)
- **Fees (SOURCED):** Maintenance technician registration costs a $111 fee to TCEQ. — [30 TAC §30.245 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/30-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-30-245). Another snippet says the Maintenance Provider license has "no minimum educational or work experience requirements", just the course plus a $111 fee. — [TCEQ OSSF licenses page, snippet](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/licensing/licenses/ossflic). **Conflict:** this contradicts the OSII / Class C / 3-year pathway above. It may be outdated text from before the rule change, or it may describe the technician registration. It must be checked directly with TCEQ.
- **Demand-for-labor signal (SOURCED):** "EPIC Septic is looking for Septic Maintenance Providers in the Austin metro area." — [search snippet via Texas Septic Guide / Indeed](https://www.indeed.com/q-aerobic-septic-jobs.html)
- **Insurance (SOURCED, generic):** Septic contractors typically carry at least $1M general liability plus commercial auto. — [Canopy Insurance Texas](https://canopyinsurancetexas.com/septic-contractor-insurance-texas/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, Williamson installed base:** 25% of roughly 230–250K households gives about 55–60K OSSFs. The household count is my assumption and is not sourced here. The aerobic share of that base is unknown.
- **ESTIMATE, unit economics:** At $300–$400 a year, 300 contracts means about 900 visits a year and $90–$120K in revenue. A route-dense operator in Hays or western Travis could plausibly run 10–15 visits a day.
- **ESTIMATE, startup cost:** Technician registration ($111) + BMP course ($340) + AMP course (price not found; assume a few hundred dollars) + GL insurance (about $1–3K a year) + test kits, sludge judge, chlorine stock and spare aerator/pump parts (about $1–3K). That totals about $3–7K if a pickup is already owned. It fits under $10K.
- **Hardest barrier:** If the licensed-MP route needs OSII, Class C, or 3 years as a technician, a newcomer's realistic model is: (a) work as a registered technician under a licensed provider, (b) partner with or buy the book of a retiring licensed provider, or (c) pursue the Class C wastewater operator license. The Class C prerequisites weren't researched.
- **What would disprove this candidate:** (1) the TCEQ license search shows many active licensed MPs based in Travis, Hays, Williamson and Bastrop, with providers advertising open capacity or discounting below about $200 a year; (2) TCEQ confirms there's no quick route to a license, so a newcomer can't hold contracts for about 3 years; (3) counties report high rates of lapsed contracts that go unenforced, which would mean the demand is elastic.

### Gaps
- Exact counts of aerobic OSSFs in Travis, Williamson, Hays and Bastrop weren't found. A TNR open-records request, or the county breakdown in the TCEQ historical data, would answer this.
- Bastrop County rules weren't confirmed beyond generic statements.
- No direct evidence of a provider shortage, such as waitlists or providers refusing new contracts, was found. Only the hiring signal.
- AMP course price and Class C wastewater operator prerequisites weren't found.

## Austin ECAD (Energy Conservation Audit and Disclosure) ordinance

### Takeaway
ECAD requires a third-party audit at sale for Austin Energy-served homes 10 or more years old. Multifamily properties (5+ units) need one audit in the year they turn 10, and it lasts 10 years. Commercial buildings over 10,000 sq ft must benchmark every year, but owners can do that themselves in ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. Audits must be done by a RESNET Rater or BPI Building Analyst. Single-family audits are cheap ($200–$300), so volume depends on home sales and on whether realtors enforce the rule.

### Cited Findings
- **Residential homes (SOURCED):** Homes at least 10 years old with fewer than four units (per the snippet) must be audited before sale, and the findings given to buyers. — [Austin Energy ECAD Residential](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/ecad-for-residential-customers). Legal basis is Austin City Code Chapter 6-7. — [Austin Energy Sellers page](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/ecad-for-residential-customers/sellers)
- **Multifamily (SOURCED):** 5 or more units. The audit is due in the calendar year the property turns 10 and is valid for 10 years. Results must go to current and prospective residents. — [Austin Energy ECAD Multifamily](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/for-multifamily-properties)
- **Commercial (SOURCED):** Buildings over 10,000 sq ft must benchmark each year via ENERGY STAR Portfolio Manager. The deadline is June 1, and letters go out in March. — [WegoWise / Austin Energy commercial page](https://www.wegowise.com/compliance/austin-energy-conservation-audit-and-disclosure-ordinance); [Austin Energy ECAD Commercial](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/for-commercial-buildings)
- **Who can audit (SOURCED):** Qualified ECAD Energy Professionals, meaning certified RESNET Raters or BPI Building Analyst Professionals registered with Austin Energy. Austin Energy publishes a PDF list. — [Austin Energy Energy Professionals](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/energy-professionals)
- **Prices (SOURCED):** About $200–$300 for a typical home of 1,800 sq ft or less with one AC system. Auditors set their own prices. — [Austin Energy FAQs](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance/ecad-for-residential-customers/faqs). Multifamily ECAD costs $250–$3,000 depending on unit count. — [Austin AES pricing](https://austinaes.com/pricing/)
- **Penalties (SOURCED):** Fines of $500–$2,000 for non-compliance. — [Austin Energy ECAD](https://austinenergy.com/energy-efficiency/ecad-ordinance); [Envigilance (2026)](https://envigilance.com/energy-monitoring/austin-ecad/)
- **Competitors (SOURCED):** Austin Auditors, Austin AES, ECAD Specialist, A+ AC, and others. — [Austin Auditors](https://austinauditors.com/services/ecad/); [Austin AES](https://www.austinaes.com/); [ECAD Specialist](https://www.ecadspecialist.com/service/ecad-audits); [A+ AC](https://www.aplusac.com/energy-audit-ecad-audit/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** Recurring demand per property is low: once per sale, or once per decade for multifamily. Volume is tied to Austin Energy-territory resales, which fall when the market cools.
- **ESTIMATE, entry cost:** BPI Building Analyst training plus exam, or RESNET rater training plus probationary ratings, plus blower door and duct test equipment, probably totals $3–8K. That's feasible under $10K but unverified.
- Commercial benchmarking isn't a required third-party service, but some owners outsource it. That could be a low-cost add-on service.
- **What would disprove this candidate:** a short wait for audits (next-day availability from many firms), an Austin Energy list showing dozens of active professionals, or data showing sellers often skip the audit without being fined.

### Gaps
- Number of registered ECAD Energy Professionals (the list PDF couldn't be fetched).
- Compliance and enforcement rates for residential sales.
- Exact certification costs (RESNET/BPI) and whether ECAD residential audits require blower door or duct-leakage testing.
- Whether any 2023–2025 Texas preemption law affects ECAD enforcement wasn't checked.

## SWPPP inspections under TCEQ Construction General Permit (TXR150000)

### Takeaway
Every construction site covered by the permit (disturbing 1 acre or more, or part of a larger common plan) needs a documented inspection every 14 days plus within 24 hours of 0.5 inches of rain, or every 7 days. Texas requires no license, only "qualified personnel". CESSWI/CPESC credentials are optional. That makes it the lowest-barrier recurring mandated service found. Several regional firms already serve Austin. Per-inspection pricing wasn't found.

### Cited Findings
- **Frequency (SOURCED):** At least once every 14 calendar days plus within 24 hours of a rain event of 0.5 inches or more. Alternatively, a 7-day schedule may be chosen. — [TCEQ 2023 CGP TXR150000](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/permitting/stormwater/general/construction/2023-cgp-txr150000.pdf) (snippet); [Sure SWPPP](https://sureswppp.com/texas-swppp-services/swppp-inspections/). An Austin-focused page says every 7 days plus after 0.5 inches of rain "as required by Travis County and the Texas CGP". — [ProSWPPP Austin (2026)](https://proswppp.com/austin-swppp-requirements/)
- **Records (SOURCED):** Written records of each inspection, kept for 3 years after the Notice of Termination. — [Projexiv](https://projexiv.com/swppp-compliance-texas-construction-sites/)
- **Qualifications (SOURCED):** "Qualified personnel" familiar with BMPs. CPESC/CESSWI is recommended but not mandated by state rules. Local jurisdictions may add requirements. — [EcoPermitPros guide](https://www.ecopermitpros.com/post/tx-swp3-inspections-guide); [ProSWPPP certification article](https://proswppp.com/swppp-certification-epa-course-cpesc-or-state-credential/)
- **Permit fee (SOURCED):** $225 for projects over 5 acres or part of a larger common plan. "Several thousand dollars" total compliance per typical project. — [ProSWPPP Texas requirements (2026)](https://proswppp.com/texas-swppp-requirements/)
- **Austin competitors (SOURCED):** Red River Storm Water, ProjectCompli (advertises CESSWI-certified inspectors), Sure SWPPP, Smart SWPPP, TX Erosion Inspections, Compliance Resources Inc, InstaSWPPP. — [Red River](https://www.redriverstormwater.com/); [ProjectCompli Austin](https://www.projectcompli.com/austin-swppp/); [Smart SWPPP Austin](https://www.smartswppp.com/markets/austin-swppp-services/); [TX Erosion Inspections](https://www.txerosioninspections.com/); [Compliance Resources](https://www.complianceresourcesinc.com/certified-swppp-inspections/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** About 26 routine inspections a year per active site, plus rain events. Sites last months to years, so a book of 20–40 active sites gives steady weekly route revenue. Startup is a tablet or inspection software, a vehicle, and GL plus E&O insurance, likely $2–6K. Optional CESSWI costs extra (price not found).
- Upsell: SWPPP plan writing, BMP installation and repair (silt fence, rock berms), and Notice of Termination filing.
- **What would disprove this candidate:** per-inspection prices commoditized below about $75–100 by national app-based firms, or large Austin GCs doing inspections in-house with superintendents (allowed as "qualified personnel"), leaving only small builders who are price-sensitive.

### Gaps
- Per-inspection or monthly prices in Austin weren't found. All provider pages quote on request.
- The number of active CGP authorizations in Travis, Williamson and Hays wasn't found. The TCEQ Central Registry could be queried.
- Whether City of Austin or Travis County require a certified inspector wasn't confirmed.

## Registered Accessibility Specialist (RAS) / Texas Accessibility Standards (TAS)

### Takeaway
State law makes this mandatory per project: every Texas project costing $50,000 or more must be registered with TDLR and needs a RAS plan review and a final inspection. There were 24,686 project registrations statewide in FY24. TDLR has actively recruited RASes, which signals supply is thin. The registration fee is only $300, but a degree or qualifying background plus a state exam are required.

### Cited Findings
- **Legal basis and threshold (SOURCED):** Projects with an estimated cost of $50,000 or more must be registered with TDLR under TAS. The owner must designate a RAS for plan review and final inspection. — [Access Plan Review](https://www.accessplanreview.com/); [Texas Access FAQ](https://www.texasaccess.com/information/texas_access_faq/)
- **Volume (SOURCED):** FY24 EAB program: 24,686 project registrations and an overall population of 25,252. — [TDLR EAB at a Glance](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/media/pdf/EAB%20at%20a%20Glance.pdf)
- **Pricing (SOURCED):** One provider charges a flat $800 for projects under $3M, covering review and inspection. The state filing fee is $175 per project. — [Access Plan Review](https://www.accessplanreview.com/); [TDLR AB fee schedule](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfees.htm)
- **RAS license fees (SOURCED):** $300 application, $250 renewal. The license lasts 1 year, and all requirements including the exam must be completed within 1 year of applying. — [TDLR AB fees](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/abfees.htm); [TDLR Apply for RAS](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/apply-ras-license.htm)
- **Requirements (SOURCED, partial):** A degree, a state exam and continuing education are required. The APA Texas Accessibility Academy offers 16 CE hours. — [O*NET license info](https://www.onetonline.org/link/licenseinfo?id=48-RAS); [APA Academy](https://www.apaaccess.org/texas-accessibility-academy/)
- **Shortage signal (SOURCED, dated 2019):** TDLR published a recruitment flyer titled "Texas needs you to become a Registered Accessibility Specialist". — [TDLR RAS recruitment flyer, 5-13-19](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ab/info/RAS%20recruitment%205-13-19.pdf) (title only; the body couldn't be fetched)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** Austin metro is one of Texas's biggest construction markets, so a meaningful share of the 24.7K registrations are local. At about $800 per project, 100 projects a year is about $80K. Startup is a license, exam, TAS training, measuring tools (digital level, force gauge, tape measures) and E&O insurance, probably under $5K. It's "recurring" only through steady project flow, not repeat visits to the same property.
- **What would disprove this candidate:** the TDLR RAS CSV showing many active RASes in the Austin area, flat-fee prices falling well below $800, or education requirements that exclude people without architecture, engineering or construction degrees.

### Gaps
- Exact RAS eligibility rules (degree types, experience), exam fee, required TDLR training course and cost.
- Current count of active RASes (the TDLR CSV is blocked) and Austin-area project counts.

## Commercial kitchen grease traps and hood cleaning (City of Austin / fire code)

### Takeaway
Grease-trap pumping is required every 90 days in Austin, but it needs a City liquid-waste-hauler permit and a vacuum truck, so it doesn't fit $10K. Hood cleaning is recurring under NFPA 96 and needs no Texas license, so it fits on capital. But Austin is served by franchises and established firms, so supply isn't thin.

### Cited Findings
- **Grease traps (SOURCED):** Must be pumped at least every 90 days, or sooner if FOG and solids reach 50% or more of wetted height (§15-10-197). Waste may be removed only by a liquid waste hauler holding a City of Austin permit. Manifests must be kept 3 years. The FOG discharge limit is 200 mg/L. — [Austin Water Grease Trap Maintenance](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/grease-trap-maintenance) (snippet); [Austin Water Pretreatment FAQ](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/pretreatment-frequently-asked-questions)
- **Hood cleaning frequency (SOURCED):** NFPA 96 sets monthly cleaning for high-volume or solid-fuel kitchens, quarterly for moderate, semi-annual for low-volume and annual for seasonal. Enforced by the Austin Fire Prevention Division. — [Facilitec SW NFPA 96 guide (2026)](https://facilitec-sw.com/compliance-regulations/nfpa-96-kitchen-exhaust-cleaning-requirements-guide/)
- **Licensing (SOURCED):** Texas has no state license for hood-cleaning contractors. GL insurance and documentation are expected. IKECA certification is an industry credential. — [Facilitec SW](https://facilitec-sw.com/service-areas/texas/kitchen-exhaust-cleaning-in-austin-tx/); [Phil Ackland training](https://philacklandtraining.com/)
- **Austin reporting (SOURCED):** Fire system inspection companies must register on The Compliance Engine. 6-month kitchen hood reports, deficiencies and repairs are submitted through it. — [Austin Fire Permits](https://www.austintexas.gov/department/afd-permits) (snippet). This most likely refers to hood fire-suppression system inspection, which is SFMO-licensed, not grease cleaning. That reading is my inference.
- **Price (SOURCED, aggregator):** About $100–$150 per hour. — [Austin Air Duct Cleaning Team](https://austinairductteam.com/services/restaurant-hood-cleaning/)
- **Incumbents (SOURCED):** HOODZ, Kitchen Guard, Facilitec SW, Pro Hood Cleaning, Austin Hood Cleaning, Bowmar Industrial. — [HOODZ Austin](https://www.hoodzinternational.com/austin/); [Kitchen Guard](https://kitchenguard.com/austin/); [Pro Hood Cleaning](https://prohoodcleaning.com/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** Hood cleaning startup (pressure washer, hot-water unit or chemicals, plastic containment, ladders, stickers, $1M GL) is about $5–9K. It's night work that needs heavy labor. The mandate is inelastic, but the supply of cleaners isn't thin.
- A possible <$10K angle, not validated: grease-trap compliance monitoring and recordkeeping for restaurants, separate from hauling.
- **What would disprove a hood-cleaning entry:** many competitors with open booking and prices below about $300 per cleaning for small kitchens.

### Gaps
- Number of permitted food establishments in Austin, Austin hauler permit count and cost, and typical per-pump prices weren't found.

## Backflow testing (confirm/refute prior finding)

### Takeaway
**Confirmed.** Austin backflow testing is crowded and commoditized. One tester advertises $75 per device, and other flat rates are $95–$115. Entry cost is low but economics are weak.

### Cited Findings
- $75 test price for RPZ, DC, PVB and SVB devices — [WaterCrest Backflow](https://watercrestbackflow.net/). A $95 residential flat rate and a $115 standard test from other providers; $70–$200 per assembly typical — [FindBackflowTesters Austin](https://www.findbackflowtesters.com/programs/austin-water-backflow-testing); [Homeyou (09/2025)](https://www.homeyou.com/tx/backflow-testing-austin-costs)

- 40-hour course costs $750–$975. TCEQ application fee is $111. 24 CE hours are required for renewal. — [Bac-Flo 40-hr ($975)](https://bacflo.com/product/40-hr-bpat-certification/); [TCEQ BPAT](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/licensing/licenses/bpatlic)
- Many providers are listed (Yelp top-10 lists, AAIS, H2O & Backflow Test, Smart Earth, South Austin Irrigation). — [Yelp](https://www.yelp.com/search?find_desc=Backflow+Testing&find_loc=Austin%2C+TX)

### Inferences
- The prior finding (9+ firms, $75 tests) holds. Backflow is best as an add-on to irrigation, plumbing or OSSF work, not as a standalone business.

## Cooling towers / Legionella (Austin)

### Takeaway
Austin has a real annual mandate: cooling towers must be registered, and an inspection form is due each March 1. But inspectors must be an independent PE (mechanical or chemical) or a TDLR ACR Class A licensee with a process-cooling endorsement. That's experience-gated, so it isn't a newcomer $10K play. Texas has no statewide Legionella mandate.

### Cited Findings
- Registration is required. Annual inspection forms are due March 1, with the inspection done no more than 90 days before. Inspectors must be an independent third-party Texas-licensed mechanical or chemical engineer, or a TDLR ACR Class A licensee with a combined process cooling and refrigeration endorsement. Penalties up to $500 have applied since 2021. — [Austin Water Cooling Tower Efficiency Program](https://www.austintexas.gov/water/cooling-tower-efficiency-program); [Cooling tower & evaporative loss](https://www.austintexas.gov/department/cooling-tower-evaporative-loss); [Inspection form](https://www.austintexas.gov/sites/default/files/files/Water/Conservation/Rebates_and_Programs/CoolingTower-InspectionForm.pdf)
- Texas has no state-specific mandatory Legionella rules comparable to NYC or California. Programs follow ASHRAE 188 voluntarily. — [Envigilance (2026)](https://envigilance.com/compliance/cooling-tower-legionella/)

### Inferences
- A good add-on for an existing ACR Class A contractor, since the inspection window is January–February and the service is annual. For a newcomer, the ACR Class A experience requirement (not sourced here) is the barrier.
- **What would disprove its value:** a small registered tower count, or water-treatment vendors already bundling the inspection for free.

### Gaps
- Number of registered towers, typical inspection price, and ACR Class A experience requirement.

## Elevators, fire sprinklers/alarms, fire extinguishers

### Takeaway
All three are annual mandates, but they're gated by credentials or experience (QEI, NICET II) and served by incumbents. None fits a $10K newcomer start.

### Cited Findings
- **Elevators (SOURCED):** Must be inspected annually by a QEI. The owner files a Certificate of Compliance ($20 per unit) with TDLR within 60 days. The QEI must hold ASME or NAESAI certification and pay a $199 TDLR application fee. — [TDLR Elevator FAQ](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/elevator/elefaq.htm); [Texas QEI LLC](https://yourtexaselevator.com/tdlr-elevator/)
- **Fire sprinkler (SOURCED):** An RME-General Inspector can perform sprinkler inspection, testing and maintenance. It requires NICET Inspection & Testing of Water-Based Systems Level II plus the state statutes and rules test. Fees are $50 initial and $100 biennial renewal. The firm needs an SCR-G registration ($950 initial) and GL insurance. — [TDI fire sprinkler licensing](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/fire/fmlisprinkler.html); [Your Standing summary](https://yourstanding.com/licenses/texas/fire-sprinkler-license)
- **Fire extinguishers:** Previously found to be incumbent-dominated. Not re-researched.

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** NICET Level II and ASME QEI both usually need documented field experience, so they exclude newcomers. The Austin Compliance Engine reporting system raises compliance visibility, which favors established firms.

### Gaps
- Exact NICET II and QEI experience prerequisites, and Austin provider counts.

## Mold (TDLR), asbestos and lead (DSHS)

### Takeaway
These are mandates triggered by an event, not recurring schedules. Mold assessment is required when a hired contractor remediates 25 or more contiguous sq ft. Asbestos surveys are required before renovation or demolition permits on commercial and public buildings. Capital fits under $10K, but revenue depends on water damage and construction volume.

### Cited Findings
- **Mold (SOURCED):** A licensed Mold Assessment Consultant must write a remediation protocol before remediation of 25 or more contiguous sq ft and do post-remediation clearance. Homeowners doing their own remediation are exempt. — [TDLR Mold notifications](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldnotifications.htm); [NTX Enviro](https://ntxenviro.com/25-square-feet-mold-rule/). MAC license $500 (renewal $500), exam $60, 40+ hours of training plus education or experience, 8-hour CE each cycle, and $1M per-occurrence GL. — [TDLR MAC apply](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldconsultant-apply.htm); [TDLR exams](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/mld/mldexam.htm)
- **Asbestos (SOURCED):** SB 509 (2001) requires municipalities to verify an asbestos survey before issuing renovation or demolition permits for public and commercial buildings. Public-building surveys must be done by a DSHS-licensed inspector. — [Asbestos survey guidance (TDH form)](https://content.civicplus.com/api/assets/0ccdcebb-e095-4e8e-be25-01c275ba5423); [DSHS notification guide](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/asbestos/pdf/AsbestosNotificationInstructionGuide.pdf). DSHS asbestos inspector license costs $130. — [Texas State Records / DSHS](https://texas.staterecords.org/asbestos-certification)
- **Lead (SOURCED):** Risk Assessor certification costs $610 for 2 years plus a $50 exam fee. It requires the Inspector (24h) and Risk Assessor (16h) courses plus education or experience (for example, a bachelor's degree plus 1 year, or high school plus 3 years). — [DSHS lead certification requirements](https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/elp/PDF/certreq.pdf); [Zack Academy](https://www.zackacademy.com/texas-lead-paint-inspector-risk-assessor)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** An asbestos survey business (license, AHERA inspector course, sampling supplies, and lab fees passed through) fits under $10K. Austin's high commercial remodel volume makes it the more "mandated-flow" option of the three. Mold assessment insurance (E&O or pollution) may be expensive; that's unverified.

### Gaps
- AHERA course cost, insurance costs, and Austin counts of commercial renovation and demolition permits.

## Playground safety (CPSI) and multifamily dryer vents

### Takeaway
CPSI certification is cheap (about $580–$720 for course and exam), but a Texas legal mandate for annual third-party inspections isn't verified. The only source is an aggregator's reading of HHS Chapter 746. No Austin or Texas ordinance requiring multifamily dryer vent cleaning was found. That demand is insurance-driven, not regulatory.

### Cited Findings
- CPSI is 12 hours of class plus a 100-question exam, and the certification lasts 3 years. — [NRPA CPSI](https://www.nrpa.org/certification/CPSI/become-a-cpsi/). 2025 course and exam: $595 member / $720 non-member (Pennsylvania) and $580 (Ohio). — [PRPS 2025](https://prps.org/events/2025/25winter-a); [OPRA 2025](https://www.opraonline.org/assnfe/ev.asp?ID=48). Texas classes are offered through TRAPS. — [TRAPS CPSI](https://traps.org/education/cpsi/)
- Texas HHS Chapter 746 is said to reference the CPSI credential for daycare playground compliance, per an aggregator. The rule text wasn't verified. — [Proportional FM (search snippet)](https://www.proportionalfm.com/insights/texas-daycare-playground-inspection-cpsi); [HHS Ch. 746 PDF](https://www.hhs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/documents/doing-business-with-hhs/provider-portal/protective-services/ccl/min-standards/chapter-746-centers.pdf)
- Dryer vents: Austin uses the 2021 UMC and IRC with local amendments covering installation, but nothing on cleaning frequency was found. — [K&M Steam Cleaning (2025)](https://kandmsteamcleaning.com/2025/05/13/dryer-vent-code-requirements-in-austin-what-homeowners-need-to-know/). Association insurers recommend or require cleaning every 2–3 years. That source is a Florida law firm, so it isn't Texas evidence. — [Becker & Poliakoff](https://beckerlawyers.com/update-dryer-vent-regulations-are-being-enforced-by-the-local-fire-safety-officials/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE:** CPSI fits a $10K start easily, but without a verified mandate the demand is discretionary (liability-driven for daycares, schools, HOAs and apartments). Dryer vent cleaning should be treated as an ordinary service business, not compliance-mandated.
- **What would disprove CPSI:** the Chapter 746 text allowing staff self-inspection with no certified inspector required.

### Gaps
- Exact Chapter 746 wording and the count of licensed child-care centers in the Austin metro.
